# Fix audio, data and evaluation before architecture

SANG's next gains will come mostly from changes it can test without touching the architecture, and above all from measuring itself correctly. The largest lever with MEASURED support in a motion model is the audio representation. On the same LivePortrait-keypoint motion task, swapping the audio encoder moved HDTF Sync-C by 3.4 points ([Teller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18429)). The second is inference settings: sampler steps and audio CFG shift sync, FID and pose diversity in both published LivePortrait/motion-latent models ([FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064), [KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)). The third is data: SANG trains on roughly 5% of TalkVid's 1,244 h ([TalkVid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13618)), and its own jump from 3.7k to 12.6k clips removed overfitting. Sync-reward preference tuning (Flow-DPO) and SyncNet losses through a differentiable renderer have the largest measured sync gains in the literature, but every one of those numbers comes from pixel-space video models. For SANG they are well-motivated SPECULATION. Renderer upgrades move FID and CSIM, not lip sync, and SANG cannot size them until it renders ground-truth motion through LivePortrait on HDTF. On the research side, concurrent work covers the recipe and the controls. AVTR-1 already uses SANG's exact 42-d space and its region-balanced loss. "Motar" (arXiv 2609.10317) already does caption control and adversarial/DMD motion losses. FLOAT, KDTalker and Ditto cover emotion, diversity and real-time rendering. That leaves open: subgroup fairness with motion-vs-renderer attribution, calibrated one-to-many generation, data-scaling curves, and sync-reward post-training in motion space. None of this is comparable to published numbers until SANG re-runs its baselines on one HDTF protocol: the same method's FID differs by more than 2x between papers.

## Where SANG stands, and what "better" can mean on HDTF

SANG is closest to three published systems. KDTalker is a 43M diffusion model on LivePortrait keypoints, trained on one RTX 4090 with 64-frame windows. Ditto is a LivePortrait-motion DiT. AVTR-1 is a 153M autoregressive flow-matching model on a **42-d LivePortrait-derived vector: axis-angle rotation plus 39 expression coordinates**. That is SANG's target space, and AVTR-1 also uses region-balanced MSE, a cosine velocity loss, logit-normal timestep sampling and per-region guidance ([AVTR-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913), [KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)). SANG's design is therefore already close to the published state of the art. The recipe itself is not a contribution.

The headroom is visible only inside a single paper's table. On HDTF, KDTalker scores **LSE-C 7.326 / LSE-D 7.548 against real video at 8.243 / 6.929** in the same table. Teller scores **7.696 / 7.536 against real video at 8.094 / 6.976** ([KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963), [Teller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18429)). A good motion-space model therefore sits about 0.4 to 0.9 LSE-C below ground truth. Closing that gap is the realistic sync target. The renderer sets a separate ceiling. In KDTalker's same-generator ablation, **LivePortrait beat face-vid2vid by +1.75 LSE-C (7.326 vs 5.579)** but had slightly worse FID (9.756 vs 8.625) ([KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)). Motar's authors state that their visual quality is "capped" by the renderer: their generated motion reaches FID 45.0 against a ground-truth-motion oracle of 44.2 on MEAD ([Motar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)). SANG's TalkVid GT-motion CSIM of 0.906 is the analogous ceiling. SANG does not yet know the HDTF equivalent, and without it no FID or CSIM target means anything.

Two internal numbers need care. The validation flow loss plateau (1.146) is not a quality measure. SD3's 61-formulation study notes that flow losses "do not necessarily correlate with the quality of output samples". Changing the timestep density changes the average loss by reweighting alone, so SANG should track loss stratified over a fixed t-grid ([SD3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206)). SANG's current audio window is also correctly **±2 frames = ±80 ms**, not the ±40 ms stated in one set of notes. That is the same per-frame span as FLOAT's frame-wise AdaLN, which attends to frames l−2 to l+2 ([FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)), and a little narrower than Sonic's 0.2 s per frame ([Sonic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16331)). Widening the window is therefore a real experiment, not a bug fix.

The ordering encodes three judgements. First, **inference changes come first because they are free and their direction is measured in motion models**. But the measured effects cut both ways. Fewer steps helped sync in FLOAT and KDTalker while hurting FID and expressiveness: FLOAT's authors report "shaky head motion and a static expression" at NFE 2 ([FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)). Higher CFG raised pose std fivefold in LeapTalk while beat alignment fell, from BAS 0.723 at scale 1 to 0.650 at scale 7 ([LeapTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)). That is a pixel model, but the effect is mechanistic. SANG should pick operating points per metric on validation speakers, not on HDTF test. Its CFG scale of 2.0 already sits where FLOAT found returns saturating.

Second, **audio representation ranks above the losses, because it is the only intervention with a multi-point Sync-C effect measured in a LivePortrait-keypoint model**. Speech-probing work finds that WavLM keeps phonetic content in its upper layers, but the best single layer for phone recognition "is always lower than at least the top two layers" ([Pasad et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03929)). So SANG's last-layer choice is plausibly suboptimal, but not catastrophic. Whether Whisper or a layer mix beats it is untested for talking heads. It needs a controlled A/B.

Third, **the flashy pixel-space levers (items 6 and 9) are discounted** for three reasons. None has been shown on a motion generator. Sync-C rewards exaggeration: FantasyTalking2 found that "Sync-C tends to assign higher confidence to exaggerated lip movements" ([FantasyTalking2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11255)). And SyncNet scores move even when the lips do not: freezing head pose alone shifted Dimitra's HDTF scores from 9.42/5.69 to 9.60/5.49 ([Dimitra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17198)). Any run that optimises SyncNet directly must also report mouth-opening statistics against GT, and a small human check.

One recommendation cuts against intuition. Pushing head-motion amplitude up will likely **lower** CSIM. IMTalker attributes Ditto's higher audio-driven CSIM (0.886 vs 0.869) to smaller head motion and notes that "the CSIM metric is highly sensitive to pose variations" ([IMTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22167)). SANG's head-std ratio of about 0.95 is already near GT, far closer than GoHD, whose pose variance is about 56% of GT's on HDTF ([GoHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09296)). Motar's best Std-R is 0.704 ([Motar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)). Remaining naturalness gaps are more likely in velocity (ratio 0.9), eyes and idle frames than in pose amplitude.

## Five research directions concurrent work leaves open

The concurrent systems close off the obvious paper. **"Audio → compact motion → frozen renderer" is standard.** FLOAT, KDTalker, Ditto, Teller, IMTalker, DEMO, MoDA and AVTR-1 all use it. The individual pieces are also taken:

- **SANG's loss recipe** is essentially AVTR-1's ([AVTR-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913)).
- **Caption and text control in motion space** is Motar's: a Q-Former fuses VLM motion captions with audio. **Adversarial and DMD motion supervision through a frozen renderer** is also Motar's ([Motar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)).
- **Emotion CFG** is FLOAT's, **pose diversity** is KDTalker's, and **eye state and real-time streaming** are Ditto's.
- **Few-second personalization** is crowded: MirrorTalk, Fallingwater and TT-SAC.

A note on naming: arXiv 2609.10317 is titled "Decoupled Self-Forcing Distillation for Streaming Talking Head Generation". "Motar" appears only in its figure caption and method section, not in its title or abstract, so cite it by title.

The open ground lies where a small, cheap, decomposed model is an advantage rather than a limitation. There are five candidates, ranked. Novelty is judged against the notes' arXiv searches, which did not sweep CVPR/ICCV/ACM MM 2026 proceedings.

**1. Who does motion-space talking-head generation fail? A subgroup audit that attributes disparity to the motion generator, the renderer or the metric, plus subgroup-robust flow matching.** TalkVid showed disparities with a single pixel-space model (V-Express). Its TalkVid-trained model scores **Sync-C 4.567 in English vs 3.695 in Polish**, 3.942 for speakers aged 60+ vs 4.329 for ages 19–30, and **FID 40.7 for White vs 48.5 for Asian speakers**. Its only remedy was changing the training data ([TalkVid](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13618)). None of the five concurrent systems reports per-subgroup results. More importantly, no end-to-end pixel model can separate the three possible sources of a disparity, and SANG's decomposition can:

- render **ground-truth** LivePortrait motion per subgroup to get the renderer's share;
- compute Sync-C and FID on **real** clips per subgroup to get the evaluator's share, since SyncNet itself may be biased by language or ethnicity;
- attribute the rest to the generator.

The method half would be group-DRO or min-max fine-tuning of the flow objective. That approach balances per-group quality in image and text generators ([EGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08660)) but has never been applied to talking heads. It is feasible on one GPU: TalkVid ships the metadata and a 500-clip stratified benchmark, and the renderer stays frozen. The risk is that reviewers read it as "applying group DRO"; the three-way attribution is the defence. A renderer-free, keypoint-space sync metric validated by humans across languages makes a natural companion. It is partially pre-empted by UniSync, which accepts landmarks ([UniSync](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16357)), so frame it as "fair metrics for fair audits", not as a new metric.

**2. Calibrated one-to-many generation: is the spread of sampled motion as wide as real human variability, and no wider?** Diversity is claimed everywhere; KDTalker's headline is pose diversity ([KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)). THUNDER names the tension: "deterministic models produce high-quality lip-sync but without rich expressions, whereas stochastic models generate diverse expressions but with lower lip-sync quality" ([THUNDER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13386)). But no 2D motion-space paper treats the generator as a probabilistic forecaster. The proposal is to score samples with proper scoring rules (energy score or CRPS on keypoint trajectories), measure coverage of real takes, and split low-entropy lips from high-entropy pose. The method hook is region-dependent temperature and guidance: tight for the mouth, wide for pose. AVTR-1's per-region guidance is related but is a knob, not a calibration study. Motar reports Std-R and VC-R ratios, not calibration. Sampling many takes is nearly free in 42-d space. The notes did not sweep the motion-forecasting calibration literature, so a related-work check is needed first.

**3. Data-scaling curves for audio-to-motion, separating hours per speaker, number of speakers and demographic diversity at fixed compute.** Large-data papers report single points. TalkVid (1,244 h) trained only on a 160 h subset ([TalkVid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13618)). TalkVerse has 6.3k h ([TalkVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14938)). AVTR-1 trained on 926 h. Scaling laws exist for body motion ([ScaMo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14559), [Kimodo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15546)), but no one has measured them for faces. Only a model as small as SANG makes a 1-GPU sweep possible, and SANG's hash-based speaker split keeps evaluation stable as the cache grows. Adding a worst-subgroup axis links this directly to direction 1: does diversity or quantity close the gap? Improvement 5 in the table produces these data points anyway.

**4. Sync-reward post-training in motion space, with a reward-hacking analysis.** Every published DPO or RL talking-head result with SyncNet numbers is a pixel video DiT: Playmate2, Hallo4, FantasyTalking2, Lip Forcing. FlowPortrait shows that single-aspect rewards cause jitter and colour-drift hacking ([FlowPortrait](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00159)). A motion generator with a fast frozen renderer can draw far more samples per prompt than any of them. That makes it a clean testbed for three questions: how much of the Sync-C gain is exaggeration, whether a GT-anchored mouth-amplitude penalty prevents it, and whether rewards computed in motion space or in pixels generalise better. This direction is more engineering than the others. It is strongest as a section of direction 1 (for example, group-robust DPO) rather than as a standalone paper.

**5. Cross-lingual transfer curves in keypoint space (alternative).** Train on English only, then on growing language mixes from TalkVid's 15 languages, and evaluate leave-one-language-out. Measure lip-aperture error directly in motion space, where SyncNet's language confound does not apply, and relate transfer to phoneme-inventory overlap. MuEx already builds a 12-language set with phoneme-guided MoE and zero-shot claims ([MuEx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06612)). Its full text must be checked for a language-mix ablation before claiming novelty.

Directions 1 through 3 fit together as one paper: an audit with attribution, a robust objective, and scaling-by-diversity curves, all on TalkVid-Bench.

## HDTF numbers from different papers cannot be compared

The same method's scores shift by more than any improvement in the table above, depending on who runs the evaluation. **FLOAT's HDTF FID is 21.100 in its own Table 1 and 9.164 in IMTalker's Table 2** ([FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064), [IMTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22167)). Split, crop, resolution and FID implementation all differ. Even ground truth moves. Real HDTF video scores:

| Paper | LSE-C (sync confidence) | LSE-D (sync distance) |
|---|---|---|
| KDTalker ([KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)) | 8.243 | 6.929 |
| Teller ([Teller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18429)) | 8.094 | 6.976 |
| Dimitra ([Dimitra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17198)) | 6.82 | 8.63 |

CSIM is equally protocol-bound. KDTalker reports 0.949 on HDTF, IMTalker's table gives Ditto 0.886, and CSIM itself moves with head-pose amplitude. SANG's TalkVid GT-motion CSIM of 0.906 therefore cannot be compared with any of them. Motar evaluates on MEAD and Hallo3 subsets rather than HDTF, so it has no HDTF number to compare at all.

The consequence is procedural. SANG must **re-run its baselines under one frozen protocol**. The comparison table needs these rows:

- real video;
- ground-truth motion rendered through LivePortrait (the renderer ceiling);
- SANG;
- every baseline with obtainable weights. IMTalker's are released under Apache-2.0; release status for FLOAT, KDTalker and Ditto must be checked, and FLOAT's license materials are reported as internally inconsistent.

The protocol must fix the split, face crop, output resolution, SyncNet checkpoint and FID/FVD implementation. It should add BAS, the pose-std and velocity ratios, eye-region variance against GT, and SANG's own audio_gain. Published numbers can then appear only as cited context, never in the same column.

The paper track (direction 1) should start at step 0. Per-subgroup GT, GT-motion and generated rows on TalkVid-Bench cost almost nothing once the protocol exists. They also turn every later A/B into fairness evidence.

## Conclusion

The literature reframes SANG's problem. Architecture is not the bottleneck: SANG's design already matches the closest concurrent systems, down to its 42-d target space and its region-balanced loss. What SANG lacks is a trustworthy measurement loop, a better-chosen audio representation, and more of the data it already has access to. The most-cited "big wins" (SyncNet losses, DPO) are pixel-model results that also inflate the metric they optimise. SANG should treat them as experiments that must survive a mouth-amplitude check, not as expected gains.

The strategic opening comes from SANG's constraints. A small model with a frozen renderer is the one setup that can cheaply separate renderer, generator and evaluator effects, sample many takes, and sweep data scale on one GPU. The large concurrent systems are not built to answer the questions of who gets failed, how calibrated the motion is, and how quality scales with speaker diversity. SANG can own them.
