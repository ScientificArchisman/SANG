# Cross-domain generative-modelling recipes for a fixed-size (53M) flow-matching motion generator (SANG-M)

Scope: techniques from image / video / audio generative modelling (2023–2026, emphasis 2025–2026) that improve small diffusion / flow-matching models at FIXED parameter count, graded for transfer to SANG-M (53.4M bidirectional DiT, 8L x 512, rectified flow, v-pred, 64x42 motion frames + 10-frame prefix, frame-wise AdaLN on time+WavLM audio+reference, region-balanced MSE + velocity loss, EMA 0.995, 10 Euler steps, audio-only CFG 1.25 mouth). Key gap: lip-trajectory correlation 0.64 vs 0.82–0.85 achievable.

Tags: [M] = measured number quoted from the source; [C] = claim by authors without a directly comparable number / qualitative; [I] = my inference for SANG.
Status: COMPLETE (2026-10-07). ~25 tool calls; all arXiv IDs below were verified by fetching the paper (SPARE: abstract only). Home-domain numbers are FID-50K on ImageNet 256 unless stated otherwise.

## Representation alignment (REPA, REPA-E, VA-VAE and related)

### Takeaway
REPA-style alignment of an early DiT block to a frozen encoder's features of the clean target is the most-replicated fixed-size gain of 2024–26 (SiT-B/2 FID 33.0 -> 24.4 at 400K; iREPA adds SiT-B 49.50 -> 43.37 at 100K) and it carries over to time-aligned audio generation, though with smaller gains (HunyuanVideo-Foley DeSync 0.79 -> 0.75). iREPA shows that the benefit comes from the token-to-token similarity structure of the target. For SANG those tokens are frames, and frame-to-frame structure is what lip-trajectory correlation measures. The speed-up shrinks with model size (about 2x at SiT-B against 17.5x at XL), so a 53M model should expect a solid but not dramatic effect.

### Inferences
- [I] SANG analogue (home domain image -> transfer to motion): precompute, offline and once, frame-level features of a frozen lip-reading / audio-visual-sync VISUAL encoder (for example an AV-HuBERT-style visual front end or a SyncNet/Synchformer-style visual encoder) on the ground-truth mouth crops of every training clip, resampled to the motion frame rate. During training, project the hidden states after block 2 or 3 of 8 (about 1/4–1/3 depth: REPA's best was 8 of 24 layers, Foley's best was the shallowest unimodal layer, and Dispersive Loss uses 1/4 depth) with a 1D temporal conv (iREPA) and subtract the per-clip temporal mean of the target features (the iREPA spatial-norm analogue, which removes identity/appearance and leaves the timing contrast). Use per-frame negative cosine, λ = 0.5 (REPA saturates at 0.5), and align only the 64 generated frames, not the 10-frame prefix.
- [I] Why it fits the lip gap: SANG's val flow loss (1.146) is averaged over all 42 dims and all t, while lip correlation depends on a few mouth dims getting their timing right. A lip-reading encoder's frame-by-frame self-similarity encodes when the mouth opens and closes for each phoneme. iREPA's result says this relational structure is what REPA transfers.
- [I] Use ONE target encoder. REPA Table 2 shows even weak encoders give most of the gain, and Foley Table 6 shows that combining two encoders (EAT+ATST) was worse than no REPA on PQ. A sync-specialised encoder matches MMAudio's choice of Synchformer for its dense condition.
- [I] Target-free fallbacks if no suitable encoder exists: SPARE-style matching of the layer-k frame-by-frame affinity matrix to the affinity matrix of the clean mouth-region motion (no encoder, no parameters), or Dispersive Loss. JiT's internal classifier head (4.37 -> 4.14) does not transfer cleanly. A per-frame viseme head would leak through the audio condition, which JiT avoided only by disabling the condition before the head.
- [I] Cost on 1 GPU: one feature-extraction pass over the dataset (hours, depending on size) plus a negligible training overhead (projector of about 0.3–1M params, discarded at inference, so the deployed model stays at 53.4M). Risk: medium. Gains are smaller at small scale, encoder features may carry identity (mitigated by mean subtraction), frame rates must be resampled, and REPA-E has no analogue because SANG has no VAE.

### Gaps
- No published REPA ablation was found for talking-head or 3DMM/motion generators that use a lip-reading encoder as the target. The SANG transfer is untested.
- REPA-E small-model numbers and MoAlign (arXiv 2510.19022, motion-centric alignment for video diffusion) were not read in full. SPARE numbers come from its abstract only.

## Objective and parameterisation (x0/eps/v, t-sampling, loss weighting, contrastive FM, dispersive loss, OT couplings, self-conditioning, few-step)

### Takeaway
Two objective changes are nearly free and consistently measured: (i) non-uniform t-sampling (logit-normal: SD3 rank 5.67 -> 1.54; LightningDiT FID 16.61 -> 13.99; JiT μ-shift 14.44 -> 8.62) and (ii) a contrastive negative-velocity term (ΔFM: SiT-B/2 42.28 -> 33.39, which stacks with REPA). The prediction target (x vs ε vs v) matters only when the per-token dimension approaches the width (JiT at 768-d). SANG's 42-d tokens in a 512-wide model sit in JiT's "all combinations work" regime (64px: 3.46–3.63 FID with v-loss). Few-step methods (MeanFlow) are a latency tool, not a quality lever, for a 10-step model.

### Inferences
- [I] t-sampling for SANG: replace U(0,1) with logit-normal(m, s=1). SANG's convention (t = 1 is noise) matches SD3's, so positive m means more noise. JiT's best μ = −0.8 is in the flipped convention and in 768-d pixel space, which needs a stronger shift. Low-dimensional motion probably needs less, so sweep m ∈ {0, +0.3, +0.5}. Keep s = 1, because SD3 shows narrow distributions hurt few-step sampling (lognorm(0.5,0.6): rank 1.00 at 50 steps but 8.50 at 5 steps) and SANG uses 10 Euler steps. Mid-to-high noise is where the coarse audio-driven timing of mouth openings is decided, and that is the region logit-normal up-weights. Judge the change with lip correlation / LSE, not uniform-t val loss: SD3 eq. 18 shows that changing π(t) re-weights the loss, so uniform-t val loss can rise while samples improve. Optionally, follow LightningDiT and switch back to uniform t for the last ~40% of training.
- [I] ΔFM for SANG: L = ||v̂ − v||² − λ||v̂ − ṽ||², where ṽ is the target of another clip in the batch, with λ = 0.05 (sharp optimum, so sweep 0.02/0.05/0.08). Apply it inside the region-balanced loss, perhaps on mouth dims only. Proposed modification (not in the paper): draw negatives from the same speaker in a different audio window, so the repulsion is about audio content and not identity. This bakes into training the audio-specificity that mouth-only CFG currently supplies at inference. Cost about 0 (no extra forward pass). Risks: the repulsion pushes predictions away from the batch mean and may raise mouth amplitude, so re-tune the CFG scale. λ too large gives "degenerate distributions" (λ = 0.15: FID 19.21).
- [I] x-prediction + v-loss (the JiT recipe adapted to SANG's convention z_t=(1−t)x0+tε, v=ε−x0: the net outputs x̂0 and v̂ = (z_t − x̂0)/max(t, 0.05), and the loss stays on v) is cheap to try and gives SANG's x̂0-based velocity (frame-difference) loss a direct output. The expected gain at 42-d is small (JiT Table 2b).
- [I] A velocity-direction (cosine) loss on v (LightningDiT: 13.99 -> 12.52) is a cheap add-on, distinct from SANG's existing temporal frame-difference loss.
- [I] Dispersive Loss (in-batch InfoNCE-ℓ2 on block-2 features, λ 0.5, τ 0.5–1) is cheap. Its authors find gains grow with model size and attribute it to regularisation, so it is worth it for SANG only if train and val losses diverge.
- [I] MeanFlow / shortcut / consistency: skip for quality. 10 NFE × 53M is already cheap, and 1-NFE MeanFlow-B/2 (6.17) does not beat multi-step models.

### Gaps
- No measured ablations were retrieved this pass for minibatch-OT couplings or self-conditioning. [I] OT couplings are awkward with a dense per-frame condition (the pairing must respect the condition), so expected value is low.
- EDM/EDM2 learned loss weighting (uncertainty-based per-t weighting) was not retrieved with numbers.

## Guidance and sampling (autoguidance, CFG-Zero*, APG, guidance interval)

### Takeaway
Autoguidance has the strongest measured guidance result: guide the model with a smaller, less-trained version of the same conditional model. It took EDM2-S ImageNet-512 FID from 2.56 to 1.34, against 2.23 for CFG and 1.68 for CFG + interval, while keeping diversity. Its cheapest variant (same size, earlier checkpoint) keeps most of the gain (1.51). APG shows the part of the CFG update parallel to the prediction acts as a gain; for SANG that gain is the mouth amplitude. This suggests separating amplitude control from timing/shape correction.

- [M] EDM2 (arXiv 2312.02696, Karras et al., CVPR 2024; ImageNet-512 latent): a SMALL separate unconditional model is enough for CFG — guiding EDM2-XXL with an XS unconditional model FID 1.81 vs XXL-unconditional 1.85 (Table 4), cutting guidance cost by almost 50%. Optimal EMA length depends strongly on guidance strength (Fig. 6, EDM2-S: best no-guidance FID at 4% EMA = 2.25; at guidance 1.2–1.4 the best EMA stays short, 2–4%, while 12–14% EMA degrades to 3.75–4.55 at guidance 1.3). "it is typical that very short EMAs yield best results when sampling with guidance". — [EDM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696)
- [C] APG — Adaptive Projected Guidance (arXiv 2410.02416, Sadat et al., ICLR 2025; text-to-image / EDM2): decomposes the CFG update ΔD into a component parallel to the conditional prediction D(z,t,y) and an orthogonal component; "the parallel component primarily causes oversaturation [a 'gain' pushing values toward the extremes], while the orthogonal component enhances image quality". APG = down-weight the parallel part (η≤1) + rescale the update norm to a sphere + "reverse momentum"; claims improved FID, recall and saturation with precision comparable to CFG at no extra compute. (Exact table numbers not retrieved in this pass.) — [APG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02416)
- [M] CFG-Zero* (arXiv 2503.18886, Fan et al., 2025; flow-matching text-to-image/video: Lumina-Next, SD3, Flux, Wan-2.1; ImageNet-256 validation): two changes — (a) "optimized scale" s* that rescales the unconditional velocity (projection of v_cond onto v_uncond) before guidance, and (b) "zero-init": zero the velocity for the first few ODE steps (at pure noise) because, when the model is under-fitted, the early-step guided velocity is worse than predicting zero. Table 1 (400M DiT, ImageNet-256, FM loss): zero-init beats CFG while under-trained, e.g. epoch 80 FID 13.53 -> 12.18, epoch 20 11.00 -> 10.65, but at 160 epochs CFG 2.84 vs zero-init 2.85 — "as the velocity is trained to be more accurate, the benefit of zero-init lessens". — [CFG-Zero*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18886)
- [M] HunyuanVideo-Foley Table 5 (arXiv 2508.16930): for the dense video condition, joint self-attention over audio+video tokens then text cross-attention: DeSync 0.78 vs parallel cross-attention 0.81 vs joint self-attention only 1.05; dropping interleaved RoPE 0.79. — [HunyuanVideo-Foley](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16930)

### Inferences
- [I] Autoguidance in SANG: D = D_weak + w(D_main − D_weak), with both models given the SAME audio + reference.
  - Step 1 costs no training: use an EARLY checkpoint of the current SANG run (~1/16–1/8 of total iterations) as D_weak, sweep w ∈ [1.5, 2.5], and apply it mouth-only like the current CFG.
  - Step 2: train a smaller guide (e.g. 4 layers × 256, a few M params) for ~1/16 of the schedule, about +3–5% compute.
  - Rationale: D_main − D_weak amplifies what the main model learned late, plausibly fine audio-to-lip timing. The CFG difference (conditional − unconditional) is dominated by the mean shape and amplitude that audio implies.
  - EMA lengths of main and guide must be tuned separately (1.34 vs 1.53).
  - Untested combination: CFG for amplitude plus autoguidance for fidelity. The paper found guidance interval reduced autoguidance's benefit, so test each alone first.
- [I] APG on mouth dims: project the guidance update onto the conditional prediction (per clip, mouth dims) and set the parallel weight η (amplitude) and the orthogonal weight w (timing/shape) independently. This could make "amplitude fixed by guidance" a separate knob that does not trade off against sync. Sampling-only and free.
- [I] Guidance interval: skip guidance at the highest-noise steps (t near 1 in SANG's convention), where the update is mostly a mean shift; for example guide only for t ∈ [0.1, 0.7]. REPA's measured effect was 1.80 -> 1.42. Free to test.
- [I] CFG-Zero*: zero-init helps only under-trained models (no benefit by 160 epochs), so it is low priority. Its optimised-scale term is a free one-liner.
- [I] EDM2 shows the optimal EMA depends strongly on guidance strength, so re-select EMA whenever the guidance scale or method changes.

### Gaps
- No measured autoguidance or APG results were found for audio, motion or talking-head generators. APG's exact table numbers were not retrieved.
- Learned or adaptive guidance (learned scale schedules, guidance distillation) was not covered in this pass.

## Conditioning design for dense time-aligned conditions (AdaLN vs cross-attn vs in-context; MMAudio sync module)

### Takeaway
For dense, time-aligned conditions, the best-measured design is the one SANG already uses: frame-aligned, per-token adaLN. MMAudio's sync module reduced DeSync from 0.973 with no sync features to 0.483, and beat feeding the same features as attention tokens (0.490). The extra gains came from three places: a sync-specialised feature extractor (Synchformer), a local temporal convolution (ConvMLP: 0.533 -> 0.483) and aligned RoPE (0.509 -> 0.483). Adding the condition as in-context tokens on top of adaLN also helped in JiT (−1.20 FID). In HunyuanVideo-Foley, joint attention alone was worse (DeSync 1.05) than joint attention plus cross-attention (0.78).

### Cited Findings
- [M] MMAudio (arXiv 2412.15322, Cheng et al., CVPR 2025; home domain: video-to-audio flow matching). Conditional synchronization module = frame-aligned conditioning c_f = Upsample(ConvMLP(F_syn)) + 1*c_g, where F_syn are 24 fps Synchformer visual features, injected through per-token adaLN (scale/shift/gate per token, not broadcast) in the audio stream. Authors: cross-modal attention "aggregate[s] features via a soft distribution, which we found to hamper precision". Table 6: with sync module DeSync 0.483; "sum sync with visual" (sync features fed as tokens into joint attention) 0.490; no sync features 0.973 (FD_PaSST 70.19 / 73.59 / 69.33). So a dense, frame-aligned, per-token adaLN path halves the desynchronisation versus no sync features. — [MMAudio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15322)
- [M] MMAudio Table 6 RoPE: aligned RoPE (visual RoPE frequencies scaled by 31.25/8 so both streams share a time axis) DeSync 0.483; no RoPE 0.509; non-aligned RoPE 0.496 — "beneficial yet insufficient for good synchrony". — [MMAudio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15322)
- [M] MMAudio Table 7: ConvMLP (1D conv kernel 3 in place of linear layers in the audio/visual streams) vs MLP: DeSync 0.483 vs 0.533, FD_PaSST 70.19 vs 73.84, IS 14.44 vs 13.01 — local temporal convolution inside the transformer FFN helps both quality and sync at near-equal size. Block mix N1=4 joint + N2=8 single-modality best-balanced (N1=6,N2=3: DeSync 0.509). — [MMAudio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15322)
- [C] MMAudio uses Synchformer features chosen because Synchformer "is trained in a self-supervised manner to detect temporal misalignment between video and audio", i.e. a sync-specialised encoder for the dense condition, not a generic semantic encoder (CLIP features are used separately at 8 fps for semantics). — [MMAudio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15322)

### Inferences
- [I] SANG's main lever is what goes into the per-frame condition, not how it is injected:
  - (a) add a sync-specialised audio feature (e.g. the audio branch of an AV-sync model, or AV-HuBERT audio features) alongside WavLM;
  - (b) run the audio features through a ConvMLP / temporal conv (kernel 3–5 at the motion frame rate) before AdaLN, so each frame's modulation sees ±1–2 frames of audio context (co-articulation; the mouth often moves before the sound);
  - (c) optionally also expose audio as in-context tokens in self-attention, with RoPE aligned to the motion frame rate, so frames can attend to look-ahead audio.
- [I] Keep the AdaLN path whatever is added. Foley's "tokens only" variant was clearly worse, and MMAudio found soft attention "hamper[s] precision".
- [I] Cost: one retrain plus possible feature extraction. Parameters can be held at 53.4M by shrinking the FFN slightly. Risk: medium, because there is no talking-head A/B evidence.

### Gaps
- No direct talking-head / audio-to-face A/B of AdaLN vs cross-attention vs in-context audio tokens was found in this pass.
- The audio-to-video sync conditioning literature (beyond the video-to-audio models MMAudio and HunyuanVideo-Foley) was not covered.

## Training dynamics (EMA / post-hoc EMA, LR schedules, batch, muP, data repetition, noise curriculum)

### Takeaway
EMA length is a first-order hyperparameter. In EDM2, FID moves from 2.56 at the best length to 5.6–9.9 at longer ones, and the optimum moves with guidance strength. Post-hoc EMA (storing snapshots) makes tuning it cheap. Bigger batch with higher LR gave a small gain (LightningDiT −0.61). Dropout hurts small models unless they overfit.

### Cited Findings
- [M] EDM2 post-hoc EMA (arXiv 2312.02696; ImageNet-512): store snapshots during training and reconstruct ANY EMA profile afterwards. Fig. 5a FID vs relative EMA length (σ_rel): final CONFIG G: 5% 7.1, 10% 2.8, 15% 2.56, 20% 5.6, 25% 9.9 — FID varies ~4x across EMA lengths; the optimum is sharp. Fig. 5c: optimum shifts toward relatively longer EMA as training progresses. Anecdotal findings: "optimal EMA length tends to go down when the model capacity is increased, and also when the complexity of the dataset is decreased"; optimal EMA ∝ 1/(α_ref² t_ref) w.r.t. LR. With post-hoc EMA, LR-decay t_ref in [30k,160k] stays within 10% of optimal FID, whereas a fixed 13% EMA varies FID by up to 72% (App. A.4). — [EDM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696)
- [M] EDM2: dropout helps only when overfitting: "enabling dropout improves our results in cases that exhibit overfitting, i.e., when the training loss continues to decrease but validation loss and FID start increasing"; enabled for M–XXL, "disabling it in the smaller configurations (XS, S) where it is harmful". — [EDM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696)
- [M] LightningDiT Table 1 (arXiv 2501.01423): batch x4 with lr x2 (same samples seen, 80 ep): FID 17.20 -> 16.59; AdamW β2=0.95: 16.59 -> 16.61 (no gain). ΔFM Table 6 (arXiv 2506.05350): REPA SiT-B/2 at fixed 400K iters: batch 256 -> 512 -> 1024 gives 42.28(?) -> 24.45 -> 22.00 (see inconsistency note above). — [LightningDiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01423); [ΔFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05350)
- [M] Autoguidance (arXiv 2406.02507) as a training-dynamics by-product: the best guide is an EARLY checkpoint (1/16 of training) of a smaller model; EMA lengths of main and guide must be tuned separately (same EMA: 1.34 -> 1.53). — [Autoguidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02507)

### Inferences
- [I] SANG's EMA 0.995 has a half-life of only about 138 steps. Keep 3–4 EMA copies (e.g. 0.995 / 0.999 / 0.9995 / 0.9999; about 214 MB each in fp32 at 53M params) or EDM2-style snapshots, and select by lip correlation under the guidance setting actually used. Essentially free.
- [I] Diagnose the 1.146 plateau first. If train loss keeps falling while val is flat, the model is overfitting: use regularisers (Dispersive Loss; dropout per EDM2's overfitting criterion) and more data. If both are flat, it is a representation/capacity limit: prioritise REPA, ΔFM and architecture.
- [I] LightningDiT warns tricks are not orthogonal: gradient clipping hurt once lognorm and velocity-direction loss were on. Re-check clipping and LR whenever t-sampling or a loss term changes.

### Gaps
- No diffusion-specific measured ablations were retrieved for μP, WSD LR schedules, weight decay on norm/AdaLN params, data repetition (multi-epoch) effects, or noise-level curricula. LightningDiT's late switch from logit-normal back to uniform t is the only curriculum evidence collected.

## Architecture at fixed size (RoPE, QK-norm, SwiGLU, registers, U-DiT skips, SSM hybrids, multi-scale)

### Takeaway
At fixed size, LLM-style blocks gave large gains in image DiTs. On DiT-XL/2 (LightningDiT): SwiGLU −2.42, RMSNorm −0.85, RoPE −2.12 FID, for −5.39 combined. On JiT-B: RoPE + qk-norm −0.79 and in-context tokens −1.20. In MMAudio, ConvMLP improved sync (DeSync 0.533 -> 0.483) and aligned RoPE helped (0.509 -> 0.483).

### Cited Findings
- [M] JiT Table 4 (arXiv 2511.13720; ImageNet 256 pixel, JiT-B/16 ~131M, 200 ep, FID-50K with CFG / with CFG interval): baseline (SwiGLU+RMSNorm) 7.48 (6.32); + RoPE, qk-norm 6.69 (5.44); + in-context class tokens (32 tokens prepended, in addition to adaLN) 5.49 (4.37). JiT-L/16: 3.39 (2.79). Footnote: removing SwiGLU+RMSNorm degrades 7.48 -> 7.89. So at equal size, RoPE+qk-norm = -0.79 FID, 32 in-context condition tokens = -1.20 FID. — [JiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13720)
- [M] LightningDiT Table 1 (arXiv 2501.01423; DiT-XL/2 latent ImageNet, 80 ep, FID no CFG), same parameter budget: + SwiGLU FFN 12.52 -> 10.10; + RMSNorm 10.10 -> 9.25; + RoPE 9.25 -> 7.13. Architecture tweaks alone = −5.39 FID at fixed size (the largest single block of gains in the table). — [LightningDiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01423)
- [M] MMAudio Table 7 (arXiv 2412.15322): ConvMLP (kernel-3 1D conv FFN) vs plain MLP in temporal streams: DeSync 0.483 vs 0.533; FD_PaSST 70.19 vs 73.84. — [MMAudio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15322)

### Inferences
- [I] If SANG uses absolute (learned or sinusoidal) positions over its 74-frame sequence, switch to 1D RoPE with qk-norm, aligned to the audio frame rate if audio tokens are added. Swap the GELU-MLP for SwiGLU at matched params (hidden ≈ 2/3 · 4d) and LayerNorm for RMSNorm (keeping AdaLN modulation). Make the FFN input projection a kernel-3 temporal conv (ConvMLP), shrinking the hidden size to stay at 53.4M.
- [I] JiT's bottleneck patch embedding does not apply: SANG's tokens are already 42-d.
- [I] For 64–74 frames, attention cost is trivial, so Mamba/SSM hybrids are unlikely to help at this length.

### Gaps
- No equal-parameter measured ablations were retrieved this pass for U-DiT / long skip connections, register tokens, Mamba/SSM hybrids or multi-scale temporal designs.
