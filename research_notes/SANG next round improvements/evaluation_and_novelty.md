## Q1. What do late-2025/2026 talking-head papers report, what do reviewers ask for, and what protocol would they accept?

### Takeaway
The 2026 baseline is still FID / FVD / CSIM / LSE-C / LSE-D on HDTF plus one more dataset. Concurrent motion-space systems routinely skip three things: human studies, out-of-domain audio, and subgroup analysis. Reviewers have started to punish small user studies and missing CSIM. A protocol that adds (i) the renderer-ceiling row SANG already has, (ii) reference-based and sequence-aligned sync metrics beyond LSE, (iii) a pairwise human study of roughly 1,000 or more judgments with anchors, and (iv) out-of-domain and per-stratum results with confidence intervals would put SANG above what the closest 2026 competitors report.

### Cited Findings
**What papers report**
- [M] AVTR-1 (2026-09-19; same 42-d LivePortrait-derived space as SANG) reports FID, FVD, CSIM, LSE-D/C and listening metrics (rPCC, PFD, SID, Var, and a new R-DGG). It evaluates on Seamless Interaction SI-184 (184 pairs, 11.90 h) against Ditto, FLOAT, SoulX Lite/Pro, AvatarForcing and DyStream. Per the fetched text, it has no human study, no OOD audio, no subgroup results, no GT-motion re-render row and no long-video test — [AVTR-1, arXiv 2609.22913](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22913)
- [M] FLOAT tests on HDTF with 78 identities × 15 s and on RAVDESS with 2 identities. Metrics: FID, 16-frame FVD, CSIM, E-FID, P-FID, LSE-D/C, LPIPS. No real-video row in its main tables; the user study and OOD results are in the appendix (C.2/C.3) — [FLOAT, arXiv 2412.01064](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [M] Temporally-aligned evaluation benchmarks 20 methods on 7 datasets (HDTF, VoxCeleb2, CelebV-HQ, MEAD and RAVDESS at 100 videos each; "Wild" 60; "Avatar" 40) with 15 metrics and no human study — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2606.01031](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01031)
- [M] TalkVid-Bench (CVPR 2026 Findings) has 500 five-second clips stratified by language (15 languages, 195 clips), ethnicity (100), gender (100) and age (5 bins, 105). It reports FID, FVD, Sync-C and Sync-D for one model, V-Express, trained on three datasets. There is no human study of generated video; humans validated only the data filter (2 annotators, Cohen's κ 0.79) — [TalkVid, arXiv 2508.13618](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13618); venue: [CVF CVPR 2026 Findings](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026F/papers/Chen_TalkVid_A_Large-Scale_Diversified_Dataset_for_Audio-Driven_Talking_Head_Synthesis_CVPRF_2026_paper.pdf)
- [M] THEval (CVPR 2026 Findings) tests on its own 5,011-clip, 6-language set (EN 1,680; FR 890; ZH 733; ES 719; JA 645; IT 344). It generated 85,000 videos from 17 models — [THEval, arXiv 2511.04520](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [M] MuEx (multilingual) built a 12-language, 95.04 h set (MTFD). It tests zero-shot on Korean, Burmese and Hindi using human rank scores: unseen-language average "5.57/5.52" vs in-set "5.68/5.37" — [MuEx, arXiv 2510.06612](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06612v2)

**Human-study designs in use (judgment counts)**
- [M] KeyFace: 51 raters × about 20 pairs of 5-s clips, same audio and identity, judged on visual quality, lip sync and motion realism. Results are Elo with bootstrapping — [KeyFace, arXiv 2503.01715](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01715)
- [M] KeySync: 1,000 pairwise comparisons, Elo with bootstrapping — [KeySync, arXiv 2505.00497](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00497)
- [M] THEval: 3,519 pairwise "which looks more realistic" ratings, Krippendorff α = 0.74 — [THEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [M] MuEx: "300 participants across 15 language groups", 20 per group, ranking 8 methods 1–8 on lip-sync consistency and teeth naturalness — [MuEx](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06612v2)
- [M] Bilabial-closure (PBM) metric paper: 16 participants × 10 audio clips — [Han et al., ACM TOG 2025, arXiv 2507.18352](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18352)
- [C] GenArena reports that "pairwise scoring universally outperforms pointwise scoring" for visual-generation evaluation, and that pointwise grading "is inherently prone to variance" (search highlight; full text not read) — [arXiv 2602.06013](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06013.pdf)

**What reviewers asked for (ICLR 2026, Microscope, rejected; all four reviewers rated 4)**
- [M] "Only 10 participants and 6 test videos per method seems not that reliable. Extensive user study is needed." Also: "no identity-similarity metrics provided"; "several baselines appear out-of-date ... e.g., Hallo2, Hallo3, OmniSync, StableAvatar"; efficiency baselines "under equal conditions"; and whether coherence holds "beyond 1600 frames". The authors conceded the user study "may prevent us from drawing statistically robust conclusions". The meta-review also called the design "conceptually incremental" — [OpenReview MOiS7FKbl2](https://openreview.net/forum?id=MOiS7FKbl2)

**Protocol-dependence of scores**
- [M] LSE changes with the audio codec alone: "average absolute difference in LSE-D and LSE-C between videos with mp4a or mpga audio is 0.4", up to 1.2 for some samples — [THEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [M] (carried over) FLOAT's HDTF FID is 21.100 in its own Table 1 and 9.164 in IMTalker's Table 2 — [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064), [IMTalker, arXiv 2511.22167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22167)

### Inferences
**[I] Proposed SANG evaluation protocol (what I expect a late-2026 CVPR/ICCV/MM reviewer to accept):**

| Tier | What | Details | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Comparability | HDTF and a second dataset (CelebV-HQ or VoxCeleb2, 100 clips as in 2606.01031) | Rows: real video, GT-motion re-render (renderer ceiling), SANG, and at least 4 baselines run by SANG under one protocol. Include at least one 2025–26 pixel model (reviewers named Hallo3, OmniSync, StableAvatar) and the motion-space peers Ditto, FLOAT and KDTalker. Metrics: FID, FVD-16 (StyleGAN-V I3D), CSIM, LSE-C/D, and PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS for self-reconstruction. Mux every video with one audio codec. | Scores move 2x across protocols, and LSE moves 0.4 with the codec. Reviewers flag missing CSIM and stale baselines. |
| B. Validity | Metrics beyond LSE | LipScore (Auto-AVSR feature cosine, reference-based) for self-reconstruction. Soft-DTW Sync-D_seq, CSIM_seq and LPIPS_seq. A lag-tolerant version of SANG's lip correlation next to frame-locked Pearson. Silence leakage (LipLeak, or THEval Silent Lip Stability). Bilabial closure (PBM-style). TMDC, for comparability with MuEx. FVD on self-supervised video features as well as I3D. BG-Flicker and SANG's background-motion metric. | THEval: LSE-C ρ = −0.164 and FID ρ = 0.210 against humans. I3D-FVD barely reacts to temporal corruption. |
| C. Long video | 1–3 min clips | CSIM per 10-s bin plus its slope; LSE per bin; jitter | Reviewers asked about coherence "beyond 1600 frames". A frozen renderer anchored on the source image should show near-zero drift, a cheap win. |
| D. OOD audio | TTS (2 or more systems), cross-identity audio, TalkVid languages and at least one unseen language, noisy audio, and kNN-VC-converted audio | Reference-free metrics only (LSE, THEval sync/silence, bilabial closure). Report the change in each metric relative to the in-domain condition. | No GT video exists for OOD audio. Neither AVTR-1 nor TalkVid reports OOD audio. |
| E. Strata | TalkVid-Bench per language, age, gender and ethnicity | Real, GT-motion and SANG rows per stratum with bootstrap 95% CIs; enlarge strata using held-out TalkVid speakers | TalkVid's strata are about 13–33 clips each (inference from 195/15, 100/3, 105/5). |
| F. Human study | Pairwise 2AFC or Good-Same-Bad, Bradley–Terry/Elo with bootstrap CIs | Three questions (lip sync, motion naturalness, visual quality). Real video and GT-motion re-render as anchors in the pool. Target 30–50 or more raters and 1,000–2,000 or more judgments over 20–30 or more distinct clips per method. Report Krippendorff α. Use native-speaker raters (MuEx used 20 per language) for every language whose lip sync is claimed. | Published comparators run 1,000–3,519 judgments. 10 raters × 6 videos was explicitly rejected as unreliable. |
| G. Meta-evaluation | Correlate every automatic metric with the human judgments at clip level | Spearman/Kendall with CIs | This turns the human study into evidence for SANG's own metrics (see Q3). |

- [I] Reporting "real-motion re-rendered" as a row is already uncommon. Neither FLOAT's main tables nor AVTR-1 has it. It is SANG's most distinctive evaluation asset.

## Q2. Documented metric weaknesses (SyncNet/LSE, FID/FVD) and validated alternatives

### Takeaway
The weaknesses are now documented with numbers. At the method level, LSE-C and LSE-D correlate negatively with human preference (ρ = −0.164 and −0.269), and FID and FVD correlate weakly (0.210 and 0.289). SyncNet ignores misalignments up to about 400 ms. LSE shifts by 0.4 with the audio codec. I3D-FVD barely reacts to temporal corruption. The validated alternatives are THEval's landmark-statistics suite (Final ρ = 0.870, but validated only across systems), LipScore (sensitivity-tested, not correlation-tested), Soft-DTW sequence metrics (no human validation) and the PBM bilabial accuracy (a small user study in 3D). No metric has been validated against humans at the clip level for 2D lip sync.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Spearman ρ with human pairwise preference across systems: THEval Final 0.870; Mouth Quality 0.765; Head Motion 0.763; Face Quality 0.699; Global Aesthetics 0.544. Baselines: LSE-C −0.164 (p=0.530), LSE-D −0.269 (p=0.297), FVD 0.289 (p=0.260), FID 0.210 (p=0.416), LMD-F 0.231 (p=0.389) — [THEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [M] THEval's 8 metrics: Global Aesthetics (TOPIQ-IAA), Mouth Quality (MUSIQ on the mouth crop), Face Quality (TOPIQ), Lip Dynamics (average std of pairwise distances among 40 lip landmarks), Head Motion Dynamics (pitch/yaw/roll plus face-centre translation variance), Eyebrow Dynamics, Silent Lip Stability (MAD of mouth opening in silences of 300 ms or more), and Lip-Sync (mean absolute difference between normalised mouth openness and audio volume) — [THEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [C] THEval says LSE is "sensitive to ... cropping, image quality and brightness", can rank generated videos above GT, and correlates negatively with humans because Wav2Lip optimises it directly. It also notes that "FLOAT and OmniAvatar tend to exaggerate mouth movements" — [THEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04520)
- [M] (carried over) FantasyTalking2: "Sync-C tends to assign higher confidence to exaggerated lip movements" — [arXiv 2508.11255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11255)
- [M] LipScore is the "cosine similarity between the generated and ground truth embeddings extracted from the final layer of a state-of-the-art lipreader" (Auto-AVSR, "21% WER" on unseen HDTF). [C] Under temporal shift, "SyncNet Confidence and Distance remain constant up to approximately 400 ms" while LipScore falls steadily; SyncNet shows "significant deviations starting at a shift of 75 pixels" of horizontal shift — [KeyFace](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01715)
- [M] LipLeak is the fraction of time the mouth is open (MAR threshold 0.25) when the face is driven by silent audio — [KeySync](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00497)
- [M] Soft-DTW sequence metrics (LPIPS_seq, CSIM_seq, Sync-D_seq, Smooth_pose_seq, Smooth_expr_seq). [C] Frame-wise metrics "disproportionately distort" scores under harmless phase shifts, and cross-correlation sync metrics "lack monotonic alignment constraints". [M] There is no human validation — [arXiv 2606.01031](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01031)
- [C] FVD content bias: "FVD increases only slightly with large temporal corruption". Self-supervised video features are "less biased toward image quality" — [Ge et al., CVPR 2024, arXiv 2404.12391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12391)
- [C] BG-Flicker is "a background-isolated metric for talking-head videos that enables more reliable evaluation of inter-frame flicker" — [FluentAvatar, arXiv 2509.12052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12052)
- [M] PBM bilabial accuracy measures the lip gap at HuBERT-labelled /p/, /b/, /m/ frames (threshold 0.15, minimum taken within ±2 frames). Models with about 99% PBM got significantly higher ratings (p<0.05; 16 participants) — [arXiv 2507.18352](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18352)
- [M] TMDC is the Pearson correlation of five scale-invariant mouth features (width, height, area, aspect ratio, openness) between real and generated video. MuEx also defines a landmark "LSE-D" (mean L2 over 26 lip landmarks). Despite the name, it is not SyncNet's LSE-D — [MuEx](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06612v2)
- [C] VSR models "succeed on different words" than humans. "a text-only n-gram baseline given only a few initial phonemes rivals human lipreading". VSR errors track training-word frequency, not visual clarity — [Jain & Harte, INTERSPEECH 2026, arXiv 2606.07435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07435)
- [C] Landmark detectors: head pose and resolution "heavily outweigh" demographic effects. Gender and race gaps vanish after controls, but "a statistically significant age-related bias" remains, with larger errors for older people — [RO-MAN 2026, arXiv 2604.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06961)
- [C] SyncNet may be English-biased, "inadequate ... for languages such as Persian, Turkish, and Arabic". This is the authors' claim; I found no controlled measurement on real video — [Computers 2025, DOI 10.3390/computers14010007](https://doi.org/10.3390/computers14010007)
- [M] A public talking-head MOS corpus exists: THQA-NTIRE, "12,247 2D and 3D talking heads" (NTIRE 2025 XGC, CVPR 2025 workshop) — [arXiv 2506.02875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02875), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zyj2000/THQA-NTIRE)

### Inferences
- [I] AV-HuBERT or Auto-AVSR WER is a risky headline metric. Language-model priors inflate it (Jain & Harte), and most public VSR models are English-only, so it cannot support multilingual claims. Use LipScore-style feature similarity for self-reconstruction and report WER only as a secondary English-only number, decoded without an external LM.
- [I] Every landmark-based metric, including THEval's, LipLeak, TMDC and SANG's rules, inherits landmark-detector bias, chiefly by age, pose and resolution. Any subgroup claim must include a real-video row per stratum so that metric bias can be subtracted.
- [I] SANG's lip-trajectory correlation (0.64 vs ceiling 0.82–0.85) is a frame-locked Pearson correlation of the TMDC family. Per 2606.01031, part of the gap may be harmless phase. Report a lag-tolerant or DTW version next to it before treating 0.64 as a deficit.
- [I] Because THEval's validation is across 17 systems, a clip-level validation, where two motions rendered by the same renderer are compared, is open and is exactly what SANG's decomposition allows.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures SyncNet LSE on real video by language, i.e. the metric's own language bias.
- The exact self-supervised feature extractor recommended by Ge et al. was not confirmed from the abstract.
- LipScore's correlation with human judgments (as opposed to sensitivity tests) was not found.

## Q3. Novelty check (2025–2026) and ranked contribution angles for SANG

### Takeaway
Two of the five earlier directions lost novelty in 2026. Sync-reward post-training is crowded (ReFree, Ditto's motion-space reward regularisation, Hallo4, FantasyTalking2). Cross-lingual transfer is covered by MuEx, with 12 languages, 3 zero-shot languages and 300 native raters. The calibrated one-to-many idea lost its method hook to ECHO (ACM MM 2026) but keeps its evaluation hook. Still open: fairness auditing of talking-head generation (no 2024–26 paper found), attribution of failures to representation, generator, renderer or metric, clip-level human validation of motion-space metrics, and data-scaling curves. SANG's strongest paper is an attribution-based, human-validated audit, built on its decomposition and renderer-ceiling rows.

### Cited Findings
- [M] An arXiv search for fairness in talking-head generation (2024–26) found no audit paper. The only demographic audits are of deepfake detection (TalkingHeadBench, WACV 2026) — [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=talking+face+fairness&searchtype=all&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=50), [arXiv 2505.24866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24866)
- [M] The only subgroup numbers come from TalkVid (V-Express trained on TalkVid-Core, 160 h). English Sync-C 4.567 vs Polish 3.695. FID: White 40.740, African 44.373, Asian 48.511. Age: 19–30 FID 37.879 and Sync-C 4.329; 60+ FID 51.141 and Sync-C 3.942. There are no real-video rows per stratum — [TalkVid](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13618)
- [M] An arXiv search for scaling in talking heads found only TalkVid's dataset-vs-dataset comparison and no scaling curves — [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=talking+head+scaling&searchtype=all&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=50)
- [M] ECHO "decomposes motion into a deterministic anchor that captures stable speech-correlated structure and a stochastic residual", with "semantic-group scaling ... across expression, jaw, and neck" (3D dyadic, ACM MM 2026) — [arXiv 2609.05506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05506)

- [C] ReFree adds "reward-free reinforcement learning ... into flow-matching training ... without relying on handcrafted synchronization metrics or reward models" (2026-06-11) — [arXiv 2606.13304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13304). - [M] MuEx: 12 languages, 95.04 h, zero-shot on Korean, Burmese and Hindi, 300 native-speaker raters, and the TMDC metric — [arXiv 2510.06612](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06612v2)

Its abstract mentions no lip-sync or face–voice consistency evaluation — [arXiv 2608.15690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15690). ### Inferences
**[I] Ranked contribution angles (1 = strongest).** GPU costs assume one GPU and SANG's existing caches. They are my estimates.

**1. Who does a talking-head system fail, and which component is responsible? A four-way attribution audit (representation, generator, renderer, metric) with a group-robust fix.**
- *Why it is not done:* no fairness audit of talking-head generation turned up (arXiv sweeps above). TalkVid's subgroup table covers one pixel model, has strata of about 13–33 clips, and has no real-video rows, so a "Polish gap" cannot be separated from SyncNet's own bias. Landmark-detector age bias (2604.06961) shows metric attribution is necessary. End-to-end pixel models (all 2026 competitors) cannot separate generator from renderer.
- *Experiment:* for each TalkVid stratum (enlarged to 50–100 clips from held-out speakers), report a ladder of rows. (a) Real video gives the metric bias. (b) Re-rendered full LivePortrait keypoints give the renderer gap. (c) Re-rendered 42-d-projected GT motion gives the representation gap; this uses SANG's measured 42-d trade-offs. (d) SANG gives the generator gap. Use bootstrap CIs. Regress on pose, resolution and lighting covariates before attributing to demographics. Then try one remedy: group-balanced sampling or group-DRO fine-tuning of the flow loss. Finish with a native-rater human check for 3–4 languages.
- *What SANG has:* the GT-motion re-render row, the decomposition, TalkVid with language metadata, the HDTF harness, renderer-free rule metrics and hash-based speaker splits.
- *Cost:* metrics and re-renders take about 1–3 GPU-days. A group-DRO fine-tune takes about 1–2 GPU-days. Human study: about 4 languages × 20 raters.
- *Main reviewer risk:* "an analysis paper / applying group-DRO". Demographic labels from classifiers are noisy and ethically sensitive. Effects may sit inside the CIs. Defence: the four-way attribution itself, plus the metric-bias table on real video, which nobody has published.

**2. Do renderer-free motion metrics agree with humans? A clip-level meta-evaluation of interpretable articulation rules, plus a per-language validity check.**
- *Why it is not done:* THEval validates landmark statistics only across systems (ρ over 17 systems). PBM was validated in 3D with 16 raters. LipScore was sensitivity-tested, not correlation-tested. TMDC and LipLeak have no human validation. Nobody has compared two motions through the same frozen renderer, which isolates motion perception from rendering quality.
- *Experiment:* (i) zero-cost validation by re-extracting keypoints from THQA-NTIRE's 12,247 MOS-rated clips and computing SANG's rules (bilabial closure, blink statistics, head-beat alignment, lip concordance) against MOS, reporting SRCC and PLCC; (ii) a lip-sync-specific pairwise study on SANG variants and perturbed GT motion (time shifts, bilabial violations, damped lips), all through the same renderer, where every rule's clip-level agreement with humans is measured next to LSE, LipScore, THEval and the Soft-DTW metrics; (iii) per-language validity, testing whether the bilabial rule transfers across TalkVid languages while rounding-type rules do not.
- *What SANG has:* the rule metrics, the frozen renderer (so controlled perturbations are trivial), the HDTF/TalkVid harness and multilingual data.
- *Cost:* compute is hours. Cost is dominated by the human study, roughly 1,500–3,000 judgments.
- *Main reviewer risk:* "THEval already did this" and "PBM already exists". Defence: clip-level, renderer-controlled, multilingual. A second risk is that THQA MOS measures overall quality, not motion, so the rules may correlate weakly there. The controlled study must carry the paper. This angle pairs naturally with #1 as "fair metrics for fair audits". Merged, the two make a strong benchmark/analysis paper.

**3. Diversity or quantity? Data-scaling curves for audio-to-motion that separate speakers, hours per speaker and languages, with a worst-group axis.**
- *Why it is not done:* the arXiv sweep shows no scaling-curve study for talking heads. TalkVid compares datasets, and its full 1,244 h is not used for training (it trains on the 160 h TalkVid-Core subset). Large 2026 systems report single points.
- *Experiment:* at fixed compute, train on {1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1}× of SANG's cache along three axes (number of speakers at fixed hours, hours at fixed speakers, number of languages). Evaluate held-out-speaker metrics, the worst-stratum gap from #1, and LipScore and rule metrics.
- *What SANG has:* a 53.4M model small enough for many runs, speaker-hash splits and the TalkVid cache. Its own 3.7k→12.6k-clip jump is a data point.
- *Cost:* the most expensive angle. About 8–12 runs × 1–2 GPU-days each, plus feature extraction if the cache grows: roughly 2–4 GPU-weeks.
- *Main reviewer risk:* the curves span too little range (under 2 orders of magnitude) to call them "scaling laws", and "more diverse data helps" may be judged obvious. Frame it as "which kind of data closes the worst-group gap" to link it with #1.

**4. Is the stochastic motion calibrated? Proper scoring rules (energy score, CRPS) per facial region, and how CFG and temperature trade calibration against sync.**
- *Why it is not done:* ECHO pre-empts the deterministic-lips/stochastic-rest method, but in 3D dyadic generation and with no calibration metrics in its abstract. FluentAvatar treats seed variance as a defect (r = 0.15). The motion-prediction convention is APD plus best-of-N, which is gameable. No talking-head paper reports CRPS or energy score. Both are proper with a single real take per condition, so no multi-take data is needed.
- *Experiment:* draw K = 16–32 samples per clip. Compute the energy score per region (lips, jaw, eyes, pose) as a function of CFG scale and noise temperature, along with rank histograms. Show that CFG makes lips over-confident or pose over-dispersed. Fit a per-region temperature/guidance schedule that improves calibration without hurting LSE or LipScore, and run a small human check that over-dispersed lips look "mumbly".
- *What SANG has:* sampling in 42-d space is nearly free, and SANG already has CFG and training-free constraint machinery.
- *Cost:* hours to about 1 GPU-day.
- *Main reviewer risk:* "why does calibration matter for talking heads?" It needs a perceptual link. ECHO must be cited and differentiated. This angle works best as a section of #2 or #4, not as a standalone main-track paper.

**5. Interpretable articulation rules as training-free sampling constraints (a neuro-symbolic angle).**
- *Why it is not done:* no constraint-guided talking-head sampler turned up. The rules exist only as metrics (PBM, LipLeak, THEval).
- *Experiment:* enforce bilabial closure at forced-aligned /p b m/ frames, mouth closure in silence and blink-rate statistics during flow sampling. Report constraint satisfaction against steps, side effects on LSE, LipScore and FID, and human preference. Evaluate on held-out metrics (LipScore, THEval, humans), not on the constrained rules.
- *What SANG has:* the constraints are already implemented.
- *Cost:* inference only, about hours.
- *Main reviewer risk:* Goodhart circularity, since the paper would optimise the same rules it scores. Training-free guidance is a known technique, so the work may read as incremental. It is best used as the method section of #2.

**6. Does the voice fit the face, and did cloning break the lips? Evaluating voice-cloned talking heads.**
- *Why it is not done:* 2608.15690 measures only SECS (per its abstract). Dubbing papers (JUST-DUB-IT, StableDub) target lip sync and identity, not face–voice congruence. FAME 2026 provides face–voice association models, but nobody applies them to generated avatars.
- *Experiment:* compare original audio with kNN-VC audio, using voices that match or mismatch the face. Report the change in LSE, LipScore and rule metrics (which should be about 0, since frame-synchronous VC preserves timing), SECS, face–voice association scores and a human 2AFC on which voice fits the face.
- *What SANG has:* kNN-VC fusion with preserved lip timing.
- *Cost:* hours.
- *Main reviewer risk:* it reads as an application or workshop paper, kNN-VC is off the shelf, and impersonation ethics need a responsible-use section. It is best as one OOD-audio row in the protocol (Q1, tier D) plus a short paper.

**Demoted from the earlier list:** (4) sync-reward post-training in motion space. ReFree and Ditto's motion-space reward regularisation reduce its novelty. Keep only the reward-hacking measurement (how much Sync-C gain is exaggeration against GT mouth amplitude and humans) as a subsection of #2. (5) Cross-lingual transfer. MuEx covers it. Fold the language axis into #1 and #3.

**Recommended packaging:** Paper A = #1 + #2 (+ #5 as the method and the reward-hacking subsection), a benchmark/analysis paper for CVPR/ICCV or the NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks track. Paper B = #3 (+ #4), if GPU time allows.
