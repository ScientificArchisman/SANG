# Motion naturalness, expressiveness and long-video consistency for motion-space talking-head generators (2024-2026)

Context for the reader: the SANG model is a non-causal flow-matching transformer that generates 64-frame windows of LivePortrait keypoint motion. Windows are chained with a 10-frame clean prefix (prefix dropout 0.5, the FLOAT recipe). It is conditioned on WavLM audio, canonical keypoints and the motion of one reference frame. It has no emotion, style, pose, blink or gaze control. Measured head-motion std ratio is about 0.95 and velocity ratio about 0.9 versus ground truth.

Evidence labels:
- **MEASURED**: a number taken from a table in the cited paper, which was retrieved and read for these notes.
- **SPECULATIVE**: inference, or abstract-level claims only.

All arXiv IDs below were checked by retrieving the paper's metadata (Firecrawl research index, 2026-09-28).

Verified IDs:
- 2412.01064 FLOAT
- 2411.19509 Ditto
- 2503.12963 KDTalker
- 2412.09296 GoHD
- 2511.06833 ConsistTalk
- 2608.00079 LeapTalk
- 2605.25488 TT-SAC
- 2609.10317: its arXiv title is "Decoupled Self-Forcing Distillation for Streaming Talking Head Generation". The method is called "Motar" only inside the paper (Fig. 1 caption, Sec. 3).

## Q1. Emotion / style / caption / intensity control

### Takeaway
Adding a coarse, sequence-level control signal is the best-evidenced way to improve expressiveness. The candidates are an emotion label with its own CFG scale, a text caption through a Q-Former, or a frame-wise intensity scalar. Removing such a branch hurts amplitude or E-FID/FVD in every ablation found. For SANG the cheapest options are:
1. FLOAT-style speech-to-emotion (S2E) label plus an incremental emotion CFG.
2. A ConsistTalk-style scalar intensity track.

Both can be trained on 1 GPU. The Motar caption branch needs a VLM captioning pass over the dataset.

### Cited Findings
- **FLOAT: speech-to-emotion label plus separate emotion CFG. MEASURED.**
  - Table 2 (HDTF / RAVDESS), removing S2E ("A - S2E"):
    - E-FID goes from 1.229/1.367 to 1.254/1.502.
    - FVD goes from 162.05/166.36 to 155.03/166.87, i.e. slightly *better* on HDTF.
    - P-FID goes from 0.032/0.031 to 0.031/0.025, i.e. slightly *better* without S2E.
    - So the label mainly helps expression (E-FID) on emotional data (RAVDESS), not head pose.
  - Replacing S2E with image-to-emotion (I2E) gives E-FID 1.158/1.305.
  - [FLOAT arXiv 2412.01064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **FLOAT: guidance-scale ablation on RAVDESS (App. Table 6). MEASURED.**

  | Audio scale γa | Emotion scale γe | E-FID | FVD | LSE-D |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 1 | 1.555 | 171.05 | 7.049 |
  | 1 | 2 | 1.334 | 166.04 | 7.212 |
  | 2 | 1 (default) | 1.367 | 166.36 | 6.994 |
  | 2 | 2 | 1.351 | 162.66 | 6.994 |

  - Higher γe improves E-FID and FVD. Higher γa improves lip sync.
  - Training uses dropout 0.1 on the reference, emotion and audio conditions (for CFG), and 0.5 on the prefix audio/motion "for smooth transition in the initial window". That is the same prefix-dropout recipe SANG uses.
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **FLOAT: user study (App. Table 7, MOS 1-5, 15 raters). MEASURED.**
  - Natural Head Motion: FLOAT 3.57±0.33, EchoMimic 3.07, AniTalker 3.00, SadTalker 2.03.
  - Natural Emotion: FLOAT 3.77, AniTalker 3.17.
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **FLOAT: sampling steps (NFE, App. Table 5, HDTF). MEASURED.**
  - E-FID by NFE: 1.542 at 2, 1.331 at 5, 1.229 at 10 (default), 1.293 at 20.
  - FVD: 178.8 at NFE 2 vs 162.1 at NFE 10.
  - The authors say low NFE gives "shaky head motion and a static expression". This supports ≥10 Euler steps for SANG.
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **FLOAT: frame-wise AdaLN vs cross-attention (Table 3). MEASURED.**
  - AdaLN gives E-FID 1.229 vs 1.452 for cross-attention, and LSE-D 7.290 vs 7.757.
  - The authors add, from videos only, that AdaLN gives "more diverse head motions".
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **Ditto: a clip-level emotion label as condition in a LivePortrait-keypoint motion DiT (Table 4, Talk9). MEASURED.**
  - Removing the emotion label:
    - FVD 219.4 → 324.1
    - CSIM 0.864 → 0.792
    - Sync-C 8.069 → 6.310
    - FID 17.25 → 23.04
  - Removing canonical keypoints: FVD 261.7, Sync-C 6.93.
  - Emotion labels were extracted from video frames, one per clip. The authors report "abrupt expression changes and exaggerated facial expressions" without the label.
  - [Ditto arXiv 2411.19509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)
- **Motar (2609.10317): motion captions plus audio fused by a Q-Former into a global query, routed separately from windowed local audio. MEASURED.**
  - Setup:
    - Captions were auto-generated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B (emotion plus head movement).
    - Text encoder: umT5-base. Audio encoder: wav2vec2-base.
    - Generator: 77M-parameter causal transformer with an MLP diffusion head.
  - Table 3 (hierarchical conditioning ablation):

    | Variant | MSE | FMD | Std-R | VC-R |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Full | 0.298 | 17.07 | 0.704 | 0.644 |
    | w/o Global (Q-Former) | 0.314 | 35.08 | 0.483 | 0.443 |
    | w/o Local audio | 0.588 | 50.44 | 0.578 | 0.282 |

  - The authors' reading: the global branch acts as "a sequence-level prior on motion intensity"; local audio controls lip alignment and pace.
  - Trained on 4×A100.
  - [Motar / Decoupled Self-Forcing arXiv 2609.10317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)
- **ConsistTalk: Audio-to-Intensity (A2I), a frame-wise intensity sequence distilled from an audio+velocity teacher. MEASURED.**
  - Table 1, HDTF, Base+OFT → Base+OFT+A2I:
    - Beat Align (BA) 1.551 → 1.624
    - Diversity 2.95 → 3.16
    - Sync-C 5.94 → 6.92
    - FVD 203.9 → 186.5
    - E-FID 1.354 → 1.376, slightly worse
  - Full model (adds IC-Init noise search): BA 1.659, Div 3.48, FVD 171.7.
  - Manipulating the intensity score at inference changes motion range qualitatively (Fig. 5c). Low intensity confines motion to the mouth.
  - Note that ConsistTalk is a Stable Diffusion/Hallo-based video model, not motion-space.
  - [ConsistTalk arXiv 2511.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06833)
- **Style codes from reference videos. SPECULATIVE / abstract only.**
  - StyleTalk++ extracts style codes for expression and head motion from reference videos (TPAMI 2024). No tables were read. [StyleTalk++ arXiv 2409.09292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09292)
  - X-Actor claims long-range emotional motion via autoregressive diffusion-forcing in a motion latent space (abstract only). [X-Actor arXiv 2508.02944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02944)
  - Dimitra claims that text-transcript features improve head-pose and expression realism (abstract only). [Dimitra arXiv 2502.17198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17198)

### Inferences
- **Recommended first step (low cost):** add an emotion one-hot from a speech emotion recognizer (FLOAT uses a wav2vec2-based speech emotion recognizer) and a separate emotion guidance scale γe. This is one extra embedding plus condition dropout, and retraining fits 1 GPU.
  - The FLOAT and Ditto evidence says E-FID and FVD improve.
  - FLOAT Table 2 says P-FID (head pose) does not improve from emotion. It will not fix the ~0.95 head-std damping.
- A scalar per-frame intensity track (ConsistTalk A2I) is the closest measured lever on head-motion amplitude and beat alignment (+0.07 BA, +0.21 Div). In SANG it could be:
  - computed from ground-truth keypoint velocity during training;
  - predicted from audio, or user-set, at inference.
  - This is SPECULATIVE for the keypoint-space transfer.
- Motar's result that removing the global query drops Std-R from 0.704 to 0.483 suggests SANG's damping may partly come from missing a sequence-level "style/intensity" prior. Even an audio-only global Q-Former token, without captions, could help (SPECULATIVE).

### Gaps
- None of the papers reports P-FID or head-std change from emotion conditioning specifically in LivePortrait keypoint space.
- No 1-GPU training-cost numbers are given for Motar's captioning pipeline.
- StyleTalk++, X-Actor and Dimitra tables were not read.

## Q2. Head pose: pose conditioning, pose generators, diversity vs realism, beat alignment

### Takeaway
Pose diversity is easy to inflate and hard to keep realistic. The evidence shows:
- CFG raises pose std monotonically but Beat Align Score (BAS) peaks at low or medium scale.
- Removing temporal attention inflates diversity through incoherent motion.
- Ground-truth-pose conditioning is a big oracle gain (FLOAT P-FID 0.032 → 0.012), which motivates a separate or explicit pose pathway.

SANG's 0.95 std ratio is already near ground truth. Most published methods sit well below ground-truth pose variance.

### Cited Findings
- **LeapTalk: audio CFG scale vs pose std and BAS (App. Table 7, HDTF). MEASURED.**

  | CFG | Yaw Std | Pitch Std | Roll Std | Avg Std | BAS |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1.0 | 1.553 | 2.653 | 0.757 | 1.655 | 0.723 |
  | 3.0 | 4.021 | 4.493 | 1.668 | 3.394 | 0.658 |
  | 5.0 | 6.840 | 5.764 | 2.396 | 5.000 | 0.696 |
  | 7.0 | 8.533 | 7.635 | 2.801 | 6.323 | 0.650 |

  - Authors: "pose diversity (Avg Std) increases with CFG scale … However, BAS gradually drops as CFG grows". BAS is actually non-monotonic (0.658 at 3.0, 0.696 at 5.0).
  - The main text instead says the default α=1.6 is best, and that "overly large guidance produces unnatural facial motion" (Fig. 8). The scale conventions between the appendix table and the main text are not reconciled in the paper.
  - LeapTalk is a one-step video-diffusion model, not motion-space.
  - [LeapTalk arXiv 2608.00079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- **LeapTalk: diversity vs baselines (App. Table 6, HDTF). MEASURED.**
  - Avg Std / BAS: Ours 4.652 / 0.696, OmniAvatar 4.043 / 0.652, SoulX-FlashHead 2.824 / 0.684, EchoMimic 1.457 / 0.650.
  - No ground-truth std is reported, so "higher is better" is unanchored.
  - [LeapTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- **KDTalker: LivePortrait implicit keypoints plus a spatiotemporal diffusion model (Table 1, HDTF). MEASURED.**
  - The diversity metric is the std of Hopenet head-pose embeddings.
  - Scores: Real video 0.639, KDTalker 0.760, SadTalker 0.494, AniTalker 0.374, AniPortrait 0.353, Real3DPortrait 0.118.
  - KDTalker *exceeds* real. The authors say it has the "smallest diversity difference" from real.
  - [KDTalker arXiv 2503.12963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)
- **KDTalker ablations (Tables 3, 4, 7). MEASURED.**
  - Removing temporal attention: diversity 2.292, LSE-C 1.360. The authors call this "excessive and incoherent head movements".
  - Removing RoPE: diversity 0.706 vs 0.760, LSE-C 7.225 vs 7.326.
  - Window length: diversity 0.673 (8 frames), 0.687 (16), 0.686 (32), 0.760 (64). LSE-C is also best at 64.
  - Generator type: a VAE generator gives diversity 0.846 but LSE-C 0.665.
  - User study (20 raters, Table 2): KDTalker chosen best for head diversity 61.7% of the time, for naturalness 45.7%.
  - Trained on a single RTX 4090; the model has 42.9M parameters.
  - [KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)
- **FLOAT: adding ground-truth 3DMM pose as a condition (Table 2, "A + 3DPose", HDTF / RAVDESS). MEASURED.**
  - P-FID 0.032/0.031 → 0.012/0.016
  - FVD 162.1/166.4 → 126.7/112.9
  - E-FID 1.229 → 0.926 on HDTF
  - This is an oracle, since pose comes from ground-truth video.
  - FLOAT also shows test-time "λ-control" editing of head direction through an orthogonal motion basis (qualitative, Fig. 6).
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **GoHD: conformer-structured diffusion pose generator ("prosody-aware"). MEASURED.**
  - Table 2 on HDTF, pose variance Var_p ×10³:
    - Ground truth 4.315
    - GoHD 2.411
    - SadTalker 2.473
    - Audio2Head 2.399
  - SSIM_p: GoHD 0.996 vs SadTalker 0.985.
  - On VoxCeleb, ground-truth Var_p is 8.746 vs GoHD 2.314. All methods are about 2-4× below ground-truth pose variance.
  - App. Table 7: removing attention from the denoiser drops SSIM_p from 0.996 to 0.983 (HDTF) and from 0.987 to 0.933 (VoxCeleb).
  - [GoHD arXiv 2412.09296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09296)
- **ConsistTalk reports Beat Align (BA, Siyao et al. 2022) together with a Diversity metric (Table 1, HDTF). MEASURED.**
  - Full model BA 1.659; FantasyTalking 1.581; Hallo 1.545; Sonic 1.41.
  - The BA scale differs from LeapTalk's BAS (~0.65-0.72), so the numbers are not comparable across papers.
  - [ConsistTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06833)
- **Ditto: horizontal-flip augmentation (MEASURED design, no ablation number).** It is used to de-bias pose toward one side, and an "adaptive loss weights" scheme balances pose vs expression groups.
  - Without the adaptive weights: Sync-C 0.289, FVD 588.8 (Table 4). Videos show "minimal facial motions".
  - [Ditto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)

### Inferences
- SANG's std ratio of ~0.95 is already far closer to ground truth than GoHD (~0.56 of ground-truth variance on HDTF, which is roughly 0.75 in std terms) or Motar (Std-R 0.704). The remaining damping is probably not the main naturalness bottleneck. Beat alignment and velocity (0.9) may matter more.
- **Recommended:** add BAS and a pose-std ratio to SANG's eval suite. Sweep audio CFG scale on validation speakers and report both (LeapTalk Table 7 protocol). Cost: inference only.
- Ditto-style per-group adaptive loss weights (pose / lips / eyes / brow) and horizontal-flip augmentation are cheap training changes. They directly target imbalanced convergence between pose and expression groups.

### Gaps
- The BAS definitions and scales differ between LeapTalk and ConsistTalk, and I found no paper reporting BAS for ground-truth HDTF alongside generated output in the same table.
- I found no controlled study of a separate pose generator vs a joint generator inside a LivePortrait keypoint model.

## Q3. Eye blink and gaze

### Takeaway
Blink and gaze are weakly correlated with audio, and explicit eye-state conditioning is the common fix. However, the only quantitative evidence found is GoHD's eye-landmark SSIM/variance. Ditto's eye-state and gaze correction are shown qualitatively only.

### Cited Findings
- **Ditto: eye-state condition. MEASURED design, qualitative effect.**
  - The eye state 𝐞 is the "aspect ratio and relative pupil position", described as "governing blinking and gaze which have weak correlations with audio".
  - It is concatenated with audio features into cross-attention.
  - Failure mode found: training used per-frame eye state, but inference used a fixed eye state from the reference, so gaze became "bound to the head pose".
  - The fix is a regression from head-pose change to gaze-offset keypoints, learned from a template video of an actor rotating the head while fixating the camera.
  - Only Fig. 6 (qualitative) is given; there is no number.
  - Ditto also maps individual LivePortrait expression dimensions to facial regions ("the 34th dimension governs the right eye's opening and closing"), enabling blendshape-like magnitude and regional control.
  - [Ditto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)
- **GoHD: gaze (pitch/yaw) as an explicit driving condition, with blinks and frowns generated in a second, temporally dependent stage. MEASURED.**
  - Table 2, eye/brow landmark variance Var_e (HDTF ×10²):
    - Ground truth 9.778
    - GoHD 5.374
    - SadTalker 2.206
    - Audio2Head 3.066
  - SSIM_e: GoHD 0.915 vs SadTalker 0.904.
  - Table 3: removing Stage 2 and the eye features drops SSIM_e from 0.915 to 0.813.
  - App. Table 6: removing the eye-motion discriminator drops SSIM_e from 0.915 to 0.840; removing the temporal eye discriminator drops it to 0.842.
  - There is a trade-off: adding Stage 2 raises mouth-landmark distance (MLD) from 1.65 to 1.79.
  - [GoHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09296)
- **TT-SAC reports enhanced "eye dynamics" (Fig. 5, qualitative) when applied to OmniAvatar. SPECULATIVE.** [TT-SAC arXiv 2605.25488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25488)
- **STEER (2607.23840) factorizes dyadic behaviour into explicit gaze, head-rhythm and emotion controls, using a causal flow-matching transformer. Abstract only; tables not read.** [STEER arXiv 2607.23840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23840)

### Inferences
- For SANG, eye aspect ratio and pupil offset are directly computable from LivePortrait keypoints or landmarks during preprocessing. They can be added as a per-frame condition with dropout.
  - At inference, the signal can be sampled from a small blink-process prior, or left to the model via condition dropout plus CFG.
  - Ditto's failure shows why: a fixed reference eye state at inference couples gaze to head pose.
- SANG's per-frame eye and brow coordinates are already generated jointly. Measuring eye-region variance vs ground truth (as GoHD Var_e does) is a cheap diagnostic. Published methods reach only 20-55% of ground-truth eye variance, so blink under-generation is a likely hidden naturalness gap (SPECULATIVE until measured).

### Gaps
- I found no paper with a numeric ablation of eye-state conditioning (with vs without) in a LivePortrait-space generator. Ditto provides only qualitative evidence.
- I found no blink-rate metric in these papers.

## Q4. Long-video generation for non-causal chunked models

### Takeaway
The evidence supports three levers:
1. **Anchoring every chunk to the reference.** LeapTalk fills non-prefix frames with the reference; its ablation shows FID 21 → 217 when removed.
2. **Periodic re-steering toward a reference motion.** Ditto m_ref; the design is described but not ablated.
3. **Training-free re-encoding of generated frames into the condition.** TT-SAC; it improves FLOAT's CSIM and FVD on every dataset.

Ditto's overlap fusion (center-weighted) is an alternative to the prefix scheme, but I found no numeric comparison of the two.

### Cited Findings
- **FLOAT chaining (the scheme SANG uses): MEASURED design.**
  - It conditions on the L′ preceding audio and generated motion latents, with 0.5 dropout on them "for smooth transition in the initial window".
  - No long-duration drift numbers are reported.
  - [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **Ditto: segment-wise fusion of generated motion (MEASURED design, no ablation).**
  - Segments overlap. "During fusion, weights are determined by the relative position of each frame within its segment, with frames closer to the segment center receiving higher weights."
  - It is adapted from progressive latent fusion. Training windows are 80 frames.
  - The reference initial motion m_ref is a condition that enforces inter-clip continuity and "periodically steer[s] the motion towards a specified target state, thereby mitigating error accumulation". An initial-motion loss L_ini ties the first generated frame to m_ref.
  - Velocity and acceleration losses (Eq. 5) are used "to enhance temporal stability".
  - Online streaming needs longer overlap (real-time factor 0.895 vs 0.635 offline; Table 6).
  - [Ditto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)
- **Ditto user study (Table 3, qualitative summary):** Ditto was "slightly weaker in the measure of naturalness of facial expressions and head movement". The authors attribute this to "the motion representation attenuating the learning of some high-frequency motion information". That representation is the same LivePortrait keypoint space SANG uses. [Ditto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)
- **LeapTalk: reference-anchored Brownian-bridge chunking. MEASURED.**
  - Each chunk input is the last K frames of the previous chunk followed by (T−K) copies of the reference (Eq. 10-11).
  - Table 2, removing the bridge: FID 21 → 217, Sync-C 8.38 → 7.16. The authors attribute identity consistency "mainly" to the bridge.
  - DINO similarity to the reference stays flat over streaming time (Fig. 5, curve only).
  - This is a video-space model; transferring it to motion space is SPECULATIVE.
  - [LeapTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- **TT-SAC: a training-free feedback loop that re-encodes generated frames to refine the conditioning, applied to FLOAT and other models. MEASURED (Table I).**

  | Dataset | Variant | CSIM | FVD | LPIPS | FID |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Hallo | Baseline | 0.7450 | 129.13 | 0.2423 | 22.57 |
  | Hallo | + identity TT-SAC | 0.7793 | 109.69 | 0.1810 | 15.63 |
  | Hallo | + motion TT-SAC | 0.7995 | 99.16 | not recorded | not recorded |
  | CelebV-HQ | Baseline | 0.6373 | 363.19 | not recorded | not recorded |
  | CelebV-HQ | + TT-SAC | 0.7050 | 243.20 | not recorded | not recorded |
  | RAVDESS | Baseline | 0.8809 | 88.70 | not recorded | not recorded |
  | RAVDESS | + TT-SAC | 0.9107 | 70.65 | not recorded | not recorded |

  - FLOAT Sync-C on Hallo: 3.486 → 3.573.
  - The best aggregation window is small (K = 1-2 frames). Larger K "may over-smooth dynamic facial variations" (Fig. 4).
  - Caveat: evaluation clips are 4-10 s. This does not measure minute-scale drift.
  - [TT-SAC arXiv 2605.25488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25488)
- **ConsistTalk: IC-Init, an inference-time noise search with background and motion-continuity rewards.** It shows Hallo drifting after 1-5 min while ConsistTalk stays stable (Fig. 5e, qualitative only). [ConsistTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06833)
- **Motar: exposure bias in autoregressive motion rollouts. MEASURED (Table 2, 256-frame rollouts).**
  - A teacher-forced 50-step model drifts (Std-R 1.001, VC-R 1.208, described as "erratic motion").
  - A 1-step consistency-distilled model collapses (Std-R 0.831, VC-R 0.435).
  - This applies to causal autoregressive chaining; a non-causal prefix-chained model has a milder form of the same exposure bias.
  - [Motar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)

### Inferences
- **Recommended (0 training cost):**
  1. Test Ditto-style overlap fusion (e.g. overlap the 10 prefix frames and cross-fade with position weights) against pure prefix hand-off.
  2. Periodically reset the "reference-frame motion" condition to the true reference, or to a running mean of generated expression, every N windows, to curb drift. This is the Ditto m_ref idea.
  3. Try TT-SAC-style K=1-2 re-encoding: replace the reference-motion condition with a blend of the reference and the last generated frames. It is training-free, and FLOAT's gains are measured, though FLOAT is FLOAT and not SANG.
- LeapTalk's "fill with reference" is analogous to initializing the non-prefix frames' noise around the reference motion rather than pure noise. That is a plausible motion-space analogue, but SPECULATIVE.
- A minutes-long drift metric is needed: per-window mean and std of keypoints vs reference over 5+ minutes, plus CSIM after rendering. The papers found use clips of 10 s or less, except the qualitative ConsistTalk and LeapTalk curves.

### Gaps
- I found no paper numerically comparing prefix chaining vs overlap fusion for a non-causal motion model.
- TT-SAC is not evaluated beyond 10 s clips.
- I found no minute-scale identity-drift numbers for FLOAT or Ditto.

## Q5. Motion diversity vs mode collapse (sampling, CFG, distribution-matching losses)

### Takeaway
The failure mechanisms are:
- **Mean-seeking regression losses.** These, and distillation, collapse amplitude.
- **Distribution-level losses.** An adversarial sequence discriminator on motion, or DMD, restores it: Std-R 0.494 → 0.590 → 0.704.
- **CFG.** Raising it raises amplitude but eventually hurts beat alignment and naturalness.
- **Too few sampling steps.** These produce shaky or static motion.

### Cited Findings
- **Motar: training-recipe ablation (Table 2, 256-frame rollouts; Std-R and VC-R ideal = 1). MEASURED.**

  | Recipe | MSE | FMD | Std-R | VC-R |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Self-forcing + L_mse | 0.259 | 29.93 | 0.494 | 0.433 |
  | + L_adv (sequence discriminator on motion latents) | 0.263 | 18.21 | 0.590 | 0.542 |
  | + L_DMD^motion (rendered-video DMD) | 0.298 | 17.07 | 0.704 | 0.644 |

  - Authors: MSE "is mean-seeking … collapsing motion amplitude and diversity".
  - They describe the adversarial loss on motion latents as "computationally inexpensive" and say it captures "long-range temporal statistics such as amplitude and rhythm".
  - MSE rises slightly "by design".
  - [Motar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10317)
- **LeapTalk (Table 7):** CFG scale 1 → 7 raises Avg pose Std from 1.655 to 6.323 while BAS falls from 0.723 to 0.650 (MEASURED). Removing audio CFG entirely: Sync-C 8.38 → 4.34 (Table 2). [LeapTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- **FLOAT (Table 5):** NFE 2 gives E-FID 1.542 vs 1.229 at NFE 10, with "shaky head motion and a static expression" (MEASURED). [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- **KDTalker (Table 4):** a diffusion generator gives diversity 0.760 with LSE-C 7.33, vs a VAE at 0.846 with LSE-C 0.665. Diversity alone is a poor objective (MEASURED). [KDTalker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)
- **GoHD: discriminators on eye-motion and coefficient sequences.** Their removal drops SSIM_e from 0.915 to about 0.84 (App. Table 6, MEASURED). This is another instance of sequence-level adversarial terms helping realism. [GoHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09296)
- **GDPO-Listener (2603.25020):** reinforcement learning with per-FLAME-group reward normalization "explicitly incentivizes high variance expressive generations" to fight regression-to-the-mean in listeners (abstract only). [GDPO-Listener arXiv 2603.25020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25020)

### Inferences
- SANG is flow-matching and non-causal, so it is less exposed than Motar's autoregressive student. Its 0.95 / 0.9 ratios already beat Motar's best (0.704 / 0.644), although the metrics are computed in different spaces.
- The most transferable, 1-GPU-cheap lever is a small temporal discriminator on 64-frame keypoint sequences, used as an auxiliary loss on the one-step x̂₀ prediction. It targets velocity (0.9) more than amplitude (SPECULATIVE transfer; the Motar evidence is causal and autoregressive).
- Cheaper still, tune at inference:
  - CFG scale per condition (separate audio and reference CFG, FLOAT incremental-CFG style);
  - number of Euler steps (≥10);
  - an initial-noise temperature slightly above 1.
  - Select using BAS, pose std ratio and velocity ratio on held-out speakers.
  - Noise temperature was not measured in any paper found (SPECULATIVE).

### Gaps
- I found no paper reporting sampling-temperature (noise-scale) sweeps for talking-head motion.
- I found no measured effect of the adversarial motion loss on a non-causal flow-matching model.

## Q6. Listening / idle behaviour and non-verbal cues

### Takeaway
Dyadic speak/listen generation is an active 2024-2026 area, but all evidence gathered here is abstract-level. The consistent message is that listener and idle motion collapse to static ("regression-to-the-mean") without explicit variance-promoting objectives or stochastic residuals.

### Cited Findings
- **INFP (2412.04037):** audio-driven dyadic head generation that switches between speaking and listening from dual-track audio, trained on the new DyConv dataset (abstract only). [INFP arXiv 2412.04037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04037)
- **DualTalk (2505.18096):** unified speaker/listener 3D talking heads, with 50 h of multi-round conversation data (abstract only). [DualTalk arXiv 2505.18096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18096)
- **GDPO-Listener (2603.25020):** identifies "listener motions collapsing into static faces" and uses GDPO reinforcement learning to reward variance (abstract only). [GDPO-Listener](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25020)
- **ECHO (2609.05506):** separates a deterministic speech anchor from a stochastic reaction residual. It states that conversational motion "benefits from decomposing stable and uncertain components rather than applying stochasticity uniformly" (abstract only). [ECHO arXiv 2609.05506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05506)
- **STEER (2607.23840):** explicit gaze, head-rhythm and emotion controls, via a causal flow-matching transformer (abstract only). [STEER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23840)
- **DyStream (2512.24408):** a flow-matching autoregressive model with a 60 ms look-ahead for dyadic streaming (abstract only). [DyStream arXiv 2512.24408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24408)

### Inferences
- For SANG, silent segments within HDTF-like data are the "idle" case. A quick diagnostic is to measure the pose and eye std ratio restricted to low-energy audio frames. If it is much lower than 0.95, idle collapse is present (SPECULATIVE).
- ECHO's anchor-plus-stochastic-residual idea is consistent with applying CFG or intensity only to the non-lip dimensions.

### Gaps
- None of these papers' tables were read, so no numbers are available here.
