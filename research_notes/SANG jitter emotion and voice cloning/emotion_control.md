# Emotion control for SANG-M (audio-driven LivePortrait-keypoint flow-matching DiT, unlabelled TalkVid)

Status: IN PROGRESS (written incrementally; sections marked TODO are not yet researched).
Date: 2026-09-28. Evidence grades used below: [M] = measured in a paper table / official repo; [R] = reported claim without a table (text, README); [S] = our speculation / inference.

## 1. FLOAT, Ditto, KDTalker, JoyVASA: how motion-space generators handle emotion

### Takeaway
FLOAT feeds a 7-class speech-emotion *softmax* into the same frame-wise AdaLN condition path as audio and adds a nested emotion CFG term (gamma_a=2, gamma_e=1). The user can override the audio's emotion by passing a one-hot label, but this is shown only qualitatively, and it works because acted RAVDESS is in the training mix. Removing speech emotion mainly hurts the emotional test set (RAVDESS E-FID 1.367 -> 1.502, LSE-D 6.994 -> 7.222); on HDTF the effect is negligible. Ditto is the closest data-regime match: clip-level HSEmotion pseudo-labels on ~50 h of in-the-wild broadcast video, and a user label or label mixture at inference (default Neutral). Removing its emotion condition hurt every metric (SyncC 8.069 -> 6.310). KDTalker and JoyVASA have no emotion control.

### Cited Findings
**FLOAT (arXiv 2412.01064, verified; architecture is the closest published analogue to SANG-M: flow matching + frame-wise AdaLN transformer in a motion latent space)**
- [M/R] Emotion signal: "a pre-trained speech emotion predictor [56] that produces softmax probabilities of seven distinct emotions: angry, disgust, fear, happy, neutral, sad, and surprise, which we then input into the vector field predictor" — the full 7-d softmax (soft label), not argmax. — [FLOAT §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] Injection: the driving condition concatenates Wav2Vec2.0 audio features a^{1:L}, the 7-d emotion label w_e, and the source motion latent; adds sinusoidal flow-time embedding; a linear "ToCondition" layer maps to c_t in R^{L x h}; a "ToScaleShift" linear produces frame-wise AdaLN (gamma, beta) and gating (alpha). "Note that w_e and w_{r->S} are shared across the L'+L frames" — i.e. emotion is a clip-global vector broadcast to every frame, entering the same per-frame AdaLN path as audio (exactly SANG-M's conditioning topology). — [FLOAT §4.2, Eq.10-11](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] Compositional (nested) CFG over audio and emotion, Eq. 14: v~ = v(c without {a,e}) + gamma_a [v(c without e) - v(c without {a,e})] + gamma_e [v(c) - v(c without e)]. Defaults gamma_a = 2, gamma_e = 1. — [FLOAT Eq.14](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [M] Guidance ablation on RAVDESS (Table 6, appendix B.3): (gamma_a, gamma_e) = (1,1): FID 33.066, FVD 171.047, E-FID 1.555, LSE-D 7.049; (1,2): 31.844 / 166.041 / 1.334 / 7.212; (2,1) default: 31.681 / 166.359 / 1.367 / 6.994; (2,2): 32.253 / 162.658 / 1.351 / 6.994. Authors: "increasing gamma_a leads to better temporal consistency (FVD) and lip synchronization quality (LSE-D)... increasing gamma_e improves video consistency (FVD) and expressiveness (E-FID)". Note: at gamma_a=1, raising gamma_e from 1 to 2 worsened LSE-D 7.049 -> 7.212; at gamma_a=2 raising gamma_e left LSE-D unchanged (6.994). — [FLOAT Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [M] Removing speech-to-emotion (S2E) (Table 2, HDTF / RAVDESS): FLOAT full FID 21.100/31.681, FVD 162.052/166.359, E-FID 1.229/1.367, P-FID 0.032/0.031, LSE-D 7.290/6.994; "A - S2E": FID 21.235/32.035, FVD 155.032/166.866, E-FID 1.254/1.502, P-FID 0.031/0.025, LSE-D 7.264/7.222. So S2E helps mostly on the emotional set (RAVDESS E-FID 1.502 -> 1.367, LSE-D 7.222 -> 6.994); on neutral HDTF the effect is within noise (FVD even better without S2E). "A - S2E + I2E" (image-to-emotion, frame-wise): 31.609 FID, 162.369 FVD, 1.305 E-FID, 7.150 LSE-D on RAVDESS. No emotion-accuracy metric is reported anywhere in FLOAT. — [FLOAT Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] User override: "the generated emotion-aware motion can be modified by redirecting the speech-driven emotion label toward a different emotion at inference time... particularly beneficial for manual redirection when the emotion predicted from speech is complex or ambiguous" (Fig. 8: "Redirecting the unclear emotion prediction to a desirable one-hot encoding, which can be further intensified by the CFV"). Only qualitative evidence (figure + video), no quantitative accuracy of redirection. — [FLOAT §5.4, Fig.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] Fig. 20: increasing gamma_e from 0 to 2 with predicted label "disgust 99.99%" -> "emotion-related expressions and motions are enhanced" (qualitative). Low NFE (2) makes "emotions ... exaggerated". — [FLOAT App. B.3/D](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] Training data: emotion only learnable because RAVDESS (24 actors, 22 train / 2 test, "14 emotional intensities") is mixed with HDTF (230 train identities, 11.3 h). "Since FLOAT learns diverse emotions in the emotion-intensive data distribution [RAVDESS], the generated emotion-aware motion can be modified by redirecting..." — i.e. redirection works because a labelled/acted emotional dataset is in the training mix. Single A100, 2,000k steps, ~2 days, batch 8, L = 50 + L' = 10 frames. — [FLOAT §5.1-5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] "as people do not always speak with a single, clear emotion, determining emotions solely from audio is often ambiguous... we inject the emotions together with other driving conditions at training phase and modify them at inference phase." — [FLOAT §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)

**Ditto (arXiv 2411.19509, verified; ACM MM 2025; LivePortrait implicit keypoints, DiT, in-the-wild broadcast data — the closest published analogue to SANG-M's data regime)**
- [R] Labels are pseudo-labels from an image FER: "We also extract emotions [29] from video frames and employ a unique emotion label s at the clip level, establishing a direct and controllable correspondence instead of simply learning the audio-to-expression mapping." Ref [29] = Savchenko, "HSEmotion: High-speed emotion recognition library", Software Impacts 2022. — [Ditto §3.2.1 + refs](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [R] Injection: eye state e, canonical keypoints c_ref and emotion s are "Enhanced Conditional Signals ... aligned temporally with audio features a, followed by channel-wise concatenation before feeding into a cross-attention module"; m_ref is concatenated with the noise. — [Ditto §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [M] Ablation (Table 4, Talk9): Full model FID 17.254, FVD 219.368, CSIM 0.864, SyncC 8.069, SyncD 7.114; "w/o Emo": FID 23.036, FVD 324.078, CSIM 0.792, SyncC 6.310, SyncD 8.990. I.e. removing the (pseudo-labelled) emotion condition hurt every metric including lip sync (SyncC -1.76). Caveat: Ditto's reconstruction-style eval extracts emotion from the ground-truth video, so "w/ Emo" leaks GT expression information; it is evidence that an emotion code *explains variance* in in-the-wild motion, not evidence of controllability. — [Ditto Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [R] Training data: "about 50 hours of broadcast scene videos from 330 identities"; 80-frame segments at 25 fps; 8 A100s, batch 1024, 500 epochs. — [Ditto §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [M, code] Official repo: the emotion vector is 8-d in HSEmotion/AffectNet-8 order ('Angry','Disgust','Fear','Happy','Neutral','Sad','Surprise','Contempt'); a user label is converted by `_get_emo_avg(idx)`: logits 8 at the chosen class(es), 0 elsewhere, then softmax (-> ~0.998 on one class, or an even mix when several indices are given, e.g. [3,4] = happy+neutral). Default `emo = 4` (Neutral). The condition handler also accepts an `[m, 8]` per-frame array ("emo arr, e.g. real") or a list of label sets per segment, and concatenates the 8-d vector per frame with the audio feature. So Ditto supports a user label, label mixtures, and time-varying emotion at inference, although the paper trains with a clip-level label. — [ditto-talkinghead core/atomic_components/condition_handler.py](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/blob/main/core/atomic_components/condition_handler.py); [stream_pipeline_offline.py `self.emo = kwargs.get("emo", 4)`](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/blob/main/stream_pipeline_offline.py)
- [R] Ditto also documents training-free "blendshape-like" control of LivePortrait expression dimensions: "we represent expression deformation as a 63-D vector (21 keypoints x xyz). For each dimension, we apply small positive and negative offsets ... the 34th dimension governs the right eye's opening... The 58th dimension controls mouth open, analogous to the 'jaw open' blendshape", enabling regional control and magnitude clamping. — [Ditto §3.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [R] Ditto user study: "Ditto performs slightly weaker in the measure of 'naturalness of facial expressions and head movement'. We believe this is due to the motion representation attenuating the learning of some high-frequency motion information". — [Ditto §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)

**KDTalker (arXiv 2503.12963, verified) and JoyVASA (arXiv 2411.09209, verified)**
- [R] KDTalker predicts LivePortrait expression deformation delta, s, t, R with a spatiotemporal diffusion model conditioned on a Wav2Lip AudioEncoder feature + reference-guided priors; the passages read describe no emotion input. It motivates LivePortrait keypoints as "capturing nuanced facial movements driven by speech and emotion" but gives no control. — [KDTalker §3.1-3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12963v1)
- [R] JoyVASA (LivePortrait-based, diffusion transformer on audio) has no emotion condition; its conclusion lists as future work "refining the control over facial expressions". — [JoyVASA §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09209)

### Inferences
- [S] FLOAT's recipe maps almost one-to-one onto SANG-M: both are rectified-flow / OT flow matching over windows with a prefix, with frame-wise AdaLN on a summed per-frame condition. Adding a soft emotion vector to the condition sum and a third CFG branch is an estimated ~1-2 day change. The open risk is not architecture but data: FLOAT learned redirection from acted RAVDESS, and TalkVid has almost no strong emotions.
- [S] Ditto's large "w/o Emo" drop (FID 17.3 -> 23.0, SyncC 8.07 -> 6.31) is probably inflated by GT leakage: the label is extracted from the evaluated video. Still, it shows that clip-level FER pseudo-labels explain real variance in in-the-wild LivePortrait motion. This is the case for pseudo-labelling TalkVid even though most of it is neutral.
- [S] Ditto's inference encoding (softmax of logits 8 on the chosen class(es), or an even mixture over several) is a ready-made way to turn a user label into the same soft-probability format that the pseudo-labeller produced at training time. Intensity could then be expressed by temperature, i.e. interpolating between the neutral distribution and the one-hot label.

### Gaps
- FLOAT's speech-emotion model [56] was not identified by name in the passages read. Its accuracy on in-the-wild speech is unknown.
- Neither FLOAT nor Ditto reports emotion-classification accuracy, so neither gives a quantitative measure of how well a user label overrides the audio.
- Ditto's training-time emotion dropout / CFG settings were not found in the paper passages read.

**SubtleTalk (arXiv 2608.06408, verified; ACM MM 2026; NOTE: 3D FLAME output, but otherwise architecturally close: WavLM + windowed flow matching + pseudo-labelled in-the-wild data + frame-wise continuous valence-arousal control)**
- [R] Data: HDTF, CelebV-HQ, VFHQ, TFHP (in-the-wild) + MEAD "to enrich emotional diversity"; SyncNet-filtered, yaw > 40 deg in >10% of frames discarded; FLAME via TEASER; Savitzky-Golay smoothing; "we extract frame-wise valence-arousal (VA) labels using EmotiEffLib (Savchenko, 2023)". Final 36,733 clips, 73.83 h, 3,905 identities. — [SubtleTalk §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [R] VA encoding: per-frame e_t = [v_t, a_t] in [-1,1]^2 Fourier-feature encoded, FE(e_t) = [e_t, {sin(2^k pi e_t), cos(2^k pi e_t)}], "since VA ... low-dimensional continuous form makes it difficult for MLPs to capture rich frequency variations". - [R] "Take it from audio" path: a VA predictor (VADP) on "a frozen emotion2vec encoder followed by temporal convolutions"; optional sparse user VA anchors are injected by feature modulation and hard-imposed on anchor frames (Eq. 17), otherwise VA is predicted from speech. — [SubtleTalk §3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [R] Lip-sync protection: a Deterministic Motion Prior (DMP) predicts expression + jaw "solely on the frozen WavLM content feature, shape β, and previous motion context ... bypassing the weak-control signals"; flow matching then models the residual. — [SubtleTalk §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [M] Ablation (Table 5; FDD upper-face dynamics / HDD head / LVE lip vertex error, all lower better): FM only 12.47 / 27.34 / 14.60; FM+DMP 11.44 / 25.57 / 12.46; FM+MultiCond (VA+intensity+prosody) 8.02 / 12.58 / **15.05**; +IDC (decoupling) 5.78 / 7.93 / 14.51; FM+DMP+MultiCond 5.21 / 6.96 / 12.59; full 4.46 / 6.61 / 11.96. **PC-Talk (arXiv 2503.14295, verified; implicit-keypoint (LivePortrait-style) deformations; emotion + speaking-style control)**
- [R] "Pure emotional deformation": D_e = CPred(emo, e_a) - CPred('neutral', e_a) — predict combined deformation for the target emotion and for neutral *from the same audio*, subtract, and add the difference, so that lip-sync deformation cancels. Ablation (Fig. 4, qualitative): "Without decomposition, facial emotions appear unnatural such as mouth over closed". ### Inferences
- [S] Across papers, a consistent pattern: explicit emotion conditioning takes Acc_emo from ~15-20% (emotion-agnostic models, near chance) to 55-85% on MEAD, at a lip-sync cost of about 0.1-0.4 SyncC. The two strongest recent results are Xemo-Talker (85.28%, LivePortrait motion, single RTX 4090) and EmoCAST (84.26%). Both train the emotion part on labelled MEAD, and Xemo-Talker does so on top of a frozen neutral model trained on unlabelled VoxCeleb/HDTF. That is structurally the same situation as SANG-M on TalkVid.
- [S] Per-frame AU conditioning beats one-hot labels by a wide margin when AUs come from ground truth (2509.19749: 78.0% vs 47.6%). That comparison is GT-leaking, and for SANG-M it would require an AU predictor from audio or text at inference. AUs are better suited as an *optional fine-grained channel* (Cafe-Talk style) than as the primary user interface.
- [S] "Take it from the audio" is weak evidence-wise. Audio-derived emotion conditions scored no better than a one-hot label (48.2% vs 47.6%), and audio carries a much weaker emotion signal than faces (DICE-Talk clustering strength ~2.4 vs 6-8). Audio-inferred emotion should therefore default to a *soft, low-intensity* setting, as MEMO's "subtle adjustment" framing suggests.
- [S] 3D-mesh papers (Cafe-Talk, SubtleTalk, CETalk) report mechanisms that transfer (label-swap test, masked CFG, VA Fourier features, lip-prior residual), but their SyncNet numbers are on MetaHuman/FLAME renders and are not comparable to 2D numbers.

### Gaps
- EDTalk (2404.01647) and EAT (2309.04946) were not read directly; their numbers here come from other papers' comparison tables (Xemo-Talker, DICE-Talk, GemTalk, 2509.19749), which disagree with one another because the protocols differ.
- Playmate, EmoCAST, CETalk and GaussianEmoTalker were only read at abstract level.
- No emotional talking-head paper trained *only* on pseudo-labelled in-the-wild data (no MEAD/RAVDESS) and reported Acc_emo. Ditto comes closest but reports no emotion accuracy.

## 3. Pseudo-labelling unlabelled in-the-wild video (FER, V-A, AUs, SER) and noise-robust training

### Takeaway
Face-based pseudo-labels are much more reliable than speech-based ones on in-the-wild data. HSEmotion reaches ~57-60% on AFEW (in-the-wild movie clips), ~63-66% on AffectNet, and gives valence-arousal from the same model. Speech emotion recognisers fall to ~42% accuracy (MEMO/emotion2vec) and ~27% UA (emotion2vec probe) on in-the-wild Mandarin M3ED, while scoring 60-100% on acted corpora. Published talking-head systems that train on pseudo-labels (Ditto: HSEmotion clip labels; SubtleTalk: EmotiEffLib frame-wise VA; FLOAT: speech-emotion softmax) all use *soft* scores or continuous VA rather than hard labels. The only documented noise handling is temporal aggregation or smoothing (MEMO majority vote over 3-s windows; EmoZone's temporal AU filtering). No talking-head paper found reports confidence thresholds or a noise-robust loss.

### Cited Findings

AFEW (in-the-wild movie clips) accuracy is ~55-60%, i.e. roughly 4 in 10 in-the-wild clip labels are wrong. **Talking-head systems trained on pseudo-labels (no human emotion annotation)**
- [R] Ditto: HSEmotion clip-level labels on ~50 h broadcast video (§1 above); FLOAT: speech-emotion softmax on HDTF+RAVDESS; SubtleTalk: EmotiEffLib frame-wise VA on HDTF/CelebV-HQ/VFHQ/TFHP (+MEAD); Cafe-Talk: rule-based AUs from blendshapes on 157 h internet video + MEAD/RAVDESS; EmoCAST builds an in-the-wild emotional talking-head dataset with emotive text descriptions (abstract). — see §1-§2 citations; [EmoCAST abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20615)

### Inferences
- [S] For TalkVid, label from the **face**, not the voice. Run HSEmotion enet_b0_8_va_mtl (8-class probabilities + valence/arousal in one 16 MB model) on the cached frames. Aggregate per window (the mean of per-frame probabilities over the 64-frame window, or over the whole clip as in Ditto), and store the *soft* 8-d vector + V/A + a confidence scalar (max-prob or 1 - entropy). The face is the target modality of the generator, so a face label supervises what the model must produce. SER would only train an audio->emotion predictor for the "take it from audio" mode.
- [S] Label noise: use the soft probabilities directly as the condition, as FLOAT and Ditto do, instead of argmax, so that ambiguous clips map near the neutral/mixed region. Add temporal smoothing (EMA or Savitzky-Golay over frames, as SubtleTalk does for motion). Optionally drop the emotion condition, i.e. treat it as null for CFG, on low-confidence windows instead of feeding a wrong label; this also produces the CFG dropout for free. These are standard pseudo-label practices, but none has a talking-head-specific ablation.
- [S] Leakage risk: HSEmotion reads expression off the same frames the generator must reproduce. A clip-level (or window-mean) label is less of a "cheat code" than a per-frame label, and it matches the user-facing control, which is a single label + intensity per utterance. A per-frame label would teach the model to copy frame expression from the label.
- [S] Class imbalance: TalkVid is "mostly neutral / mildly positive", so the pseudo-labelled distribution will be dominated by neutral/happy. Expect weak control for fear/disgust/anger unless a small labelled acted set (MEAD/CREMA-D/RAVDESS) is mixed in, as FLOAT, DICE-Talk (1:1 ratio), Cafe-Talk and SubtleTalk all do, or unless the model is trained with class-balanced sampling.
- [S] Speech-emotion recognisers are shaky cross-lingually on in-the-wild speech (M3ED ~42%), and TalkVid is 15-language. So an audio->emotion predictor for SANG-M is best trained on TalkVid itself with the *face* pseudo-labels as targets (a cross-modal distillation that SubtleTalk's VADP approximates with emotion2vec features). Off-the-shelf SER labels should not be taken at face value.

### Gaps
- No talking-head paper was found that ablates confidence-thresholding or soft-vs-hard pseudo-labels.
- emotion2vec+ large's per-dataset accuracy is published only as an image in the README. SenseVoice's SER accuracy was not retrieved in this session.
- The actual emotion distribution of TalkVid under HSEmotion is unknown; it must be measured before committing to class-based control.
- Licence caveat: HSEmotion code is Apache-2.0, but its models are trained on AffectNet (research-only licence). EmoNet is CC BY-NC-ND, and LibreFace carries a USC research licence. The commercial status of *using* these models to label training data needs legal review.

## 5. Injection and guidance in a frame-wise AdaLN DiT; keeping lip sync

### Takeaway
The best-matched precedent (FLOAT) simply concatenates a 7-d soft emotion vector to the per-frame condition before the AdaLN projection and adds a *nested* emotion CFG term; this is cheap and gives a user-override path, but every paper that measured it shows a small lip-sync cost from emotion conditioning (typically 0.1-0.4 SyncC or ~0.2 LSE-D), which the stronger systems contain with (a) a frozen or separately-trained neutral/lip path plus a residual emotion branch, and/or (b) region- or subspace-restricted emotion influence, and/or (c) mask-/scale-controlled guidance.

### Cited Findings
**Where the emotion signal enters**
- [R] FLOAT: emotion 7-d softmax concatenated with per-frame audio features + source motion latent, then linear "ToCondition" (+ flow-time embedding) -> frame-wise AdaLN scale/shift + gate; emotion is identical for all frames of the window. — [FLOAT §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] Ditto: 8-d emotion concatenated channel-wise with audio (per frame) into a cross-attention "Enhanced Conditional Signals" stream; the code accepts per-frame emotion arrays. — [Ditto §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3); [condition_handler.py](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/blob/main/core/atomic_components/condition_handler.py)
- [R] MEMO: emotion embedding via "emotion-adaptive layer normalization" in each layer; Cafe-Talk: coarse emotion label + intensity through AdaLN, with Fig. 13 showing AdaLN gives a cleaner emotion confusion matrix than a non-AdaLN variant. — [MEMO §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04448v1); [Cafe-Talk App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14517)
- [M] Global vs frame-wise emotion (FLOAT Table 2, RAVDESS): clip-global speech emotion (S2E) E-FID 1.367 / LSE-D 6.994; frame-wise image-emotion (I2E) instead: E-FID 1.305 / LSE-D 7.150; neither: 1.502 / 7.222. Frame-wise face-derived emotion slightly improves expression realism but loses some lip-sync vs clip-level speech emotion (single run, no error bars). — [FLOAT Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [R] SubtleTalk injects frame-wise VA via Fourier features and window-level regional intensity scalars via a separate global encoder; Xemo-Talker injects the label through "zero-initialized adapters" in a separate emotion branch on top of a frozen neutral model. — [SubtleTalk §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1); [Xemo-Talker](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1)

**Guidance formulations**
- [R] Nested audio/emotion CFG (FLOAT Eq. 14): v~ = v(∅) + gamma_a [v(a) - v(∅)] + gamma_e [v(a,e) - v(a)]; defaults gamma_a=2, gamma_e=1. AUHead uses the same nested structure with (audio+image) as the outer and AU as the inner condition, best AU scale 3.5 on a U-shaped FID curve. MEMO uses eps~ = (1+w) eps(a,e) - w eps(a). — [FLOAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064); [AUHead Eq.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09534v2); [MEMO Eq.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04448v1)
- [R] Mask-based (temporal) CFG, Cafe-Talk Eq. 6: M^ = G_∅ + alpha (G_{A,C} - G_∅) + beta Z ⊙ (G_{A,C,F} - G_∅), Z = per-frame mask of frames with a user condition; beta doubles as intensity. — [Cafe-Talk](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14517)
- [R] Condition-dropout schedules used to enable these: DICE-Talk 5% audio / 5% image / 5% emotion / 5% all; AUHead "each condition is randomly zeroed"; GemTalk 0.05 for reference/audio/motion frames. — [DICE-Talk §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18087v2); [AUHead §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09534v2); [GemTalk §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2)

**Measured lip-sync cost of emotion conditioning, and mitigations**
- [M] FLOAT: at gamma_a=1, gamma_e 1->2 raises LSE-D 7.049 -> 7.212; at gamma_a=2 LSE-D is flat (6.994) — keeping audio CFG at 2 absorbs the emotion guidance. — [FLOAT Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064)
- [M] AUHead: adding AU conditioning drops MEAD Sync 6.9885 -> 6.6311 (2D LMK) while improving FID/LMD. Xemo-Talker: emotion branch changes LSE-D 8.02 -> 8.21 while Acc_emo 15.84 -> 85.28. GemTalk: Sync-C 7.125 -> 7.027 while Acc_emo 17.1 -> 59.3. SubtleTalk: multi-condition affect controls raise LVE 14.60 -> 15.05 without the lip prior/decoupling, full model 11.96. — [AUHead Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09534v2); [Xemo-Talker Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1); [GemTalk Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2); [SubtleTalk Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [M] Subspace restriction: Xemo-Talker's emotion loss on the lowest-variance 10% PCA directions of LivePortrait motion gives LSE-C 6.51 / LSE-D 8.14 / Acc 80.51 vs whole-space 6.28 / 8.34 / 75.84. — [Xemo-Talker Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1)
- [R] Region restriction: EmoZone-Talker's learnable region attention bias (mouth zone favours audio, upper face favours AUs); PC-Talk's emotion = CPred(emo) - CPred(neutral) from the same audio so lip deformation cancels; SubtleTalk's DMP predicts jaw/expression from content audio only. — [EmoZone-Talker §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15848v2); [PC-Talk §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14295v3); [SubtleTalk §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [R] Reference leakage: MEMO reports that the emotional tone is "largely inferred from the facial expression of the reference image" and uses emotion-decoupling training (reference frame drawn from a *different-emotion* clip of the same person) so the label can override it; ExpPortrait notes implicit-keypoint methods including LivePortrait "entangle expression dynamics with identity attributes". — [MEMO §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04448v1); [ExpPortrait §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19900)

### Inferences
- [S] SANG-M's reference condition includes "a random frame's 42-d motion", which carries that frame's expression. On in-the-wild data where emotion is roughly constant within a clip, this reference frame is a strong emotion cue and will compete with (and partly defeat) any emotion label — the MEMO problem. Mitigations: (i) draw the reference motion from a different clip of the same speaker when available (TalkVid has ~1.6 clips/speaker, so often not possible), (ii) drop the reference motion at a higher rate when an emotion label is present, or (iii) neutralise the reference motion (subtract the speaker's per-clip mean expression offset).
- [S] Because SANG-M already uses frame-wise AdaLN with a summed condition, FLOAT's recipe transfers with ~zero architectural change: add an emotion embedding (Linear(8 -> d) on a soft probability vector, plus an intensity scalar) to the per-frame condition sum, independent dropout ~0.1-0.2 on the emotion token, and a third CFG term. The nested order (audio outer, emotion inner) matches FLOAT and AUHead.
- [S] Lip-sync protection options in increasing cost: keep gamma_a >= 2 (FLOAT evidence); apply emotion guidance only to non-lip dims (a per-dimension mask on the emotion CFG delta, analogous to Cafe-Talk's temporal mask but over SANG-M's 42 output dims — lips 6,12,14,17,19,20 excluded or down-weighted); two-stage training with the current model frozen and a zero-init emotion adapter (Xemo-Talker); or PC-Talk-style "emotion minus neutral" differencing at inference, which costs one extra forward pass.

### Gaps
- No paper found that ablates *per-dimension/region-masked CFG* in a motion-space generator; the dimension-masked emotion CFG proposed above is untested.
- None of the lip-sync costs above come with seeds/error bars; differences of 0.1-0.2 SyncC may be within run-to-run noise.

## 6. Training-free emotion offsets in LivePortrait keypoint space

### Takeaway
Training-free editing of LivePortrait expression coordinates is established practice (Ditto's per-dimension blendshape-like offsets, PC-Talk's neutral-subtracted emotional deformation, GemTalk's scaled-and-clamped blendshapes), but no paper measures a naive "add the mean happy-minus-neutral delta" baseline in LivePortrait space; the closest evidence (PC-Talk's decomposition ablation, ExpPortrait's offset ablation) is qualitative and says naive offsets are either unnatural at the mouth or muted.

### Cited Findings
- [R] LivePortrait: driving keypoints = canonical keypoints x head pose + expression deformation; eye and lip deformation offsets are decoupled "allowing them to be linearly added"; compact implicit keypoints "can effectively represent a kind of blendshapes". — [LivePortrait arXiv 2407.03168](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03168v1)
- [R] Ditto: per-dimension ± offsets on the 63-D (21 keypoints x xyz) expression vector map to localised effects ("34th dimension governs the right eye's opening", "58th ... mouth open, analogous to the 'jaw open' blendshape"), used for regional restriction and magnitude clamping. — [Ditto §3.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509v3)
- [R/M] PC-Talk: D_e = CPred(emo, audio) - CPred(neutral, audio) (model-based, not training-free, but applied as an additive, scalable, region-selectable offset at inference). Ablation: without decomposition, "facial emotions appear unnatural such as mouth over closed" (qualitative Fig. 4); lip-scale edits keep sync only within ~0.8-1.2. — [PC-Talk §3.3, §4.4-4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14295v3)
- [R] GemTalk intensity control: "proportional scaling rather than additive offsets" on the top-10 emotion-correlated blendshapes, "clamped within dataset statistical extrema extended by a margin of 0.15, thereby preventing facial imbalance that would otherwise lead to control failure". — [GemTalk §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2)
- [R] DICE-Talk's "prompt" mode = class-mean of emotion-prior embeddings over many same-emotion videos (a mean-embedding, applied as a condition rather than as a keypoint offset). — [DICE-Talk §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18087v2)
- [R] ExpPortrait (SMPL-X-based, not LivePortrait): "directly applies expression offsets ... yields muted facial expressiveness" vs their learned transfer (qualitative Fig. 8). — [ExpPortrait §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19900)
- [M] Xemo-Talker's PCA of LivePortrait motion: principal components = jaw opening + rigid pose; emotion-discriminative supervision works best in the low-variance tail — suggesting emotion offsets in LivePortrait space are small-amplitude relative to articulation. — [Xemo-Talker Fig.1, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1)

### Inferences
- [S] A cheap baseline worth running before any training: from pseudo-labelled TalkVid (or MEAD, research use) compute, per emotion, the mean of (frame expression − same-speaker neutral mean) in SANG-M's 39 expression dims, *excluding or zeroing the six lip keypoints' vertical components* (to avoid the "mouth over closed" failure PC-Talk reports), add alpha x offset to generated sequences, clamp to the dataset's per-dim range (GemTalk's rule), and measure Acc_emo / LSE-C vs alpha. This gives the floor any learned method must beat.
- [S] SANG-M predicts only 13 of LivePortrait's 21 keypoints (lips, eyes, brows). Emotion cues carried by the remaining keypoints (cheeks/nasolabial region: AU6 cheek raise, AU9 nose wrinkle) stay frozen at the source photo's values, which may cap achievable expressiveness of "happy" (Duchenne smile) and "disgust". This should be checked by re-rendering real emotional MEAD frames with the 8 non-predicted keypoints frozen and measuring the classifier accuracy drop.
- [S] Because LivePortrait expression deformation entangles identity (ExpPortrait's observation), offsets should be computed relative to each speaker's own neutral mean and possibly scaled by the source's canonical keypoint spread, not as absolute population deltas.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of mean-delta emotion offsets in LivePortrait keypoint space was found.
- Whether the 13-keypoint subset suffices to express all 7-8 basic emotions is unmeasured.

## 7. Evaluation protocol (emotion classifier on rendered frames, user studies)

### Takeaway
There is no single standard: the EAT lineage reports Acc_emo from Emotion-FAN fine-tuned on MEAD-train (MEAD real-video ceiling 85.38%), the DICE-Talk/GemTalk lineage uses an EMO-AffectNet-style "Emo-Score", FLOAT reports only E-FID, and everyone adds SyncNet LSE-C/D, FID/FVD, CSIM and a 1-5 MOS or pairwise user study. Numbers are therefore not comparable across papers; SANG-M should report at least two independent classifiers plus a real-video ceiling and a label-override test.

### Cited Findings
- [R] Emotion-FAN (Meng et al. 2019) fine-tuned on the MEAD training split, top-1 Acc_emo — "Following EAT, we fine-tune Emotion-FAN on the MEAD training split"; also used by 2509.19749. Real MEAD test videos score 85.38% under this protocol; an emotion-agnostic LivePortrait-motion model scores 15.84%. — [Xemo-Talker §Experiments, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1); [2509.19749 §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19749v1)
- [R] "Emo-Score (Ryumina et al., 2022)" computed only over neutral/happy/surprised/angry, averaged, and split into Neutral vs Non-neutral (DICE-Talk); GemTalk cites the same reference but calls it "Emotion-Fan". Emotion-agnostic audio models score high on "Neutral" and near-zero on "Non-neutral" (SadTalker 0.7447 / 0.0448), so a split report exposes models that just stay neutral. — [DICE-Talk §4.2, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18087v2); [GemTalk §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2)
- [R] E-FID (expression FID from EMO/Tian et al. 2024) is used by FLOAT, GemTalk and PC-Talk as a distribution-level expression metric; FLOAT reports no classification accuracy at all. — [FLOAT Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01064); [GemTalk Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2)
- [R] Motion-space classifier: Cafe-Talk trains a 1-layer transformer emotion classifier directly on motion sequences (blendshapes) from MEAD, 70/15/15 split, 88.28% in-domain accuracy, and reports Acc + feature-std "Div". — [Cafe-Talk App. D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14517)
- [M] Label-override protocol (Cafe-Talk Table 8): paired 59.68%; label masked 23.68%; label swapped vs audio: 57.22% (label as GT) / 10.86% (audio as GT). Intensity protocol (Table 9): per-intensity subsets 51.68 / 63.86 / 66.26%, intensity=0 19.07%. — [Cafe-Talk App. D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14517)
- [R] Lip sync under emotion: SyncNet LSE-C/LSE-D (all 2D papers), M-LMD mouth-landmark distance (AUHead, 2509.19749, EAT lineage); identity CSIM (ArcFace); image FID/FVD/CPBD. — [Xemo-Talker](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1); [AUHead Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09534v2)
- [M] User-study designs: DICE-Talk 20 raters x 40 sets, 1-5 on Emotion / Lip Sync / Quality / Smoothness; GemTalk 50 participants x 100 videos, 1-5 on emotion / ID / lip sync / smoothness; PC-Talk MOS with >20 participants, 10 clips; SubtleTalk 27 participants pairwise (randomised left/right) on 10 OOD internet clips covering angry/fear/happy/sad/surprise/neutral. — [DICE-Talk Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18087v2); [GemTalk Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2); [PC-Talk Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14295v3); [SubtleTalk Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06408v1)
- [M] Classifier sensitivity caveat: the same method gets very different scores under different protocols — DICE-Talk Acc_emo 42.03% (Xemo-Talker's Emotion-FAN protocol, MEAD) vs 49.439% (GemTalk's protocol, MEAD+RAVDESS) vs Emo-Score 0.4865 (own 4-class protocol); EAT 75.43% vs 52.302% vs 0.4673. — [Xemo-Talker Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14700v1); [GemTalk Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00663v2); [DICE-Talk Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18087v2)

### Inferences
- [S] Proposed SANG-M protocol: (1) Acc_emo with two independent image classifiers on rendered frames — HSEmotion enet_b2_8 (AffectNet-trained, Apache-2.0 code) and an Emotion-FAN / EMO-AffectNet fine-tuned on MEAD-train — reported with the real-video ceiling on the same clips and a Neutral / Non-neutral split; (2) a motion-space classifier trained on LivePortrait 42-d sequences of MEAD/CREMA-D (Cafe-Talk style) — cheap, renderer-independent, but must not be the same model used to pseudo-label training data; (3) Cafe-Talk-style swap test (neutral audio + each label; emotional audio + conflicting label) reporting accuracy w.r.t. label and w.r.t. audio; (4) intensity monotonicity (Acc and classifier confidence vs intensity/guidance scale); (5) LSE-C/D and M-LMD on the *same* clips for every emotion and scale, plus TalkVid-val LSE-C to check that neutral behaviour is unchanged; (6) a pairwise user study (emotion match, lip sync, naturalness) on out-of-domain multilingual audio, since TalkVid is 15-language.
- [S] Avoid evaluating with the same recogniser used for pseudo-labelling (e.g. HSEmotion) as the only metric — it rewards overfitting to that classifier's biases.

### Gaps
- No agreed benchmark for *cross-lingual* emotional talking heads was found; all reported Acc_emo numbers are on English acted data (MEAD/RAVDESS/CREMA-D).
- EAT's original evaluation text was not read directly; its protocol is cited via Xemo-Talker.
