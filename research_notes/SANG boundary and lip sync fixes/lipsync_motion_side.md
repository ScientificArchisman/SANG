## Q1. How CFG scale and schedule affect motion amplitude and lip sync in audio-driven generators

### Takeaway
Wherever guidance scale and motion amplitude are measured together, amplitude grows roughly monotonically with the audio CFG scale, while alignment or sync quality saturates or declines.
- In a video model, head-pose std grows about 3.8x from scale 1 to 7 while beat alignment falls (LeapTalk).
- In a 3DMM motion model, CFG lowers lip-vertex error but worsens the mouth-opening difference vs GT (DiffPoseTalk).

There is no published motion-space curve of lip amplitude vs γ. The mechanism plus SANG's own numbers (std 1.06 in training vs 1.52 at γ=2) make "CFG causes over-articulation" the leading hypothesis. It should be confirmed with a free γ sweep.

### Cited Findings
- [M] (pixel / video-latent) **LeapTalk Tab. 7 (HDTF), audio CFG scale vs head-pose std and beat alignment.**
  - Setup: Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B DiT with wav2vec audio via cross-attention. CFG is `s_cfg = s_cond + (α−1)(s_cond − s_uncond)`, where uncond = zero audio. Default α = 1.6.

  | CFG scale | Yaw std | Pitch std | Roll std | Avg std | BAS |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1.0 | 1.553 | 2.653 | 0.757 | 1.655 | 0.723 |
  | 3.0 | 4.021 | 4.493 | 1.668 | 3.394 | 0.658 |
  | 5.0 | 6.840 | 5.764 | 2.396 | 5.000 | 0.696 |
  | 7.0 | 8.533 | 7.635 | 2.801 | 6.323 | 0.650 |

  - Authors: "overly large guidance produces unnatural facial motion". The BAS trend is non-monotonic but ends lower.
  - No lip-amplitude metric is reported.
  - Source: [LeapTalk, arXiv 2608.00079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- [M] (motion, 3DMM diffusion) **DiffPoseTalk Tab. 1 ablation.**
  - Setup: CFG over audio and style. Dropout sets style to null with p = 0.45, and both audio and style to null with p = 0.1. HuBERT encoder with a trainable transformer. 100-frame windows with a 10-frame overlap.

  | Variant | LVE (mm) ↓ | FDD (1e-5 m) ↓ | MOD (mm) ↓ | BA ↑ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Ours (with CFG) | 8.94 | 9.60 | 1.62 | 0.29 |
  | Ours w/o CFG | 9.58 | 9.59 | 1.56 | 0.29 |

  - CFG improves per-frame lip-vertex error by ~0.64 mm but slightly **worsens** the mouth-opening difference vs GT (1.56 → 1.62 mm).
  - Scale values are not given in the text read.
  - This is the only motion-space ablation found in which CFG moves an explicit mouth-opening-vs-GT metric.
  - Source: [DiffPoseTalk, arXiv 2310.00434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00434)
- [M] (motion) **FLOAT Tab. 6** (companion note): γa 1 → 2 moves LSE-D only 7.049 → 6.994 (RAVDESS). No amplitude metric. The small sync gain from γ above 1 is the relevant point for SANG.
  - Source: [FLOAT, arXiv 2412.01064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01064)
- [C] (motion, same 42-d LivePortrait motion space as SANG) **AVTR-1** (Kravtsov et al., "AVTR-1: Open Stack for Real-Time Interactive Avatars").
  - Motion: "three rotation coordinates, plus 39 expression coordinates (brow, eyes, mouth)". Generated by flow matching with Euler, "four model evaluations per chunk".
  - Guidance is applied **per region** in regional latent spaces (rotation, brow, eyes, mouth), built from four linear projections of 512-d decoder tokens.
  - The **past-only prediction is the CFG baseline** (Eq. 9): l̃(q) = l_p(q) + w_self(q)(l_self(q) − l_p(q)) + w_other(q)(l_other(q) − l_p(q)) + w_ref(q)(l_ref(q) − l_p(q)).
  - However, "the released runtime API uses one value per condition and applies it across all four regions". No numeric weights and no ablation of guidance were found.
  - Initial noise is **truncated** to [−τ, τ] with τ = 1.2, using temporally correlated ("progressive") noise with α = 2.
  - Guidance dropout: self-audio, other-audio and reference are dropped together for 45% of samples, and all three for 15%.
  - Audio: HuBERT-Large, 1024-d at 50 Hz.
  - Source: [AVTR-1, arXiv 2609.22913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913)
- [M]/[C] (motion) **Avatar Forcing** (motion-latent autoencoder, diffusion forcing) samples with "classifier-free guidance scale of 2" and "10 NFEs with the Euler solver". This is essentially SANG's sampling recipe. There is no CFG ablation.
  - Source: [Avatar Forcing, arXiv 2601.00664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00664)
- [C] (motion) **Ditto and KDTalker** (both LivePortrait motion space) do not discuss CFG at all.
  - Ditto: 50 vs 10 steps give "minimal quality differences".
  - KDTalker still reaches LSE-C 7.326 / LSE-D 7.548 vs real 8.243 / 6.929 (Tab. 1, HDTF). Its head-motion "Diversity" is 0.760 vs real 0.639, so it overshoots real diversity even without CFG.
  - Sources: [Ditto, arXiv 2411.19509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509); [KDTalker, arXiv 2503.12963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)
- [C] (pixel) **OmniHuman-1** applies CFG to audio and text only, at scale 6.5: "only conditions with relatively weaker motion-related influence, such as audio and text, require CFG". Its limitation note: "uncoordinated or overly expressive movements still occur".
  - A search-engine extract of the arXiv PDF also credits it with **CFG annealing**. In that account, higher CFG gives "pronounced wrinkles", lower CFG "compromises lip synchronization and motion expressiveness", and the CFG magnitude is "progressively reduce[d] ... throughout the inference process".
  - The HTML version I fetched did not show the annealing passage, and there are no numbers.
  - Source: [OmniHuman-1, arXiv 2502.01061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01061)
- [M]/[C] (pixel, metric bias) **FantasyTalking2**: "Sync-C tends to assign higher confidence to exaggerated lip movements, whereas human annotators consistently favor natural, fluid articulation".
  - Sync-C agrees with human lip-sync preference 72.34% of the time, against 86.94% for their Qwen2.5-Omni reward model (Tab. 2).
  - Consequence: any γ that "improves" Sync-C may be buying it with amplitude.
  - Source: [FantasyTalking2, arXiv 2508.11255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11255)
- [M] (pixel, companion note) **Lip Forcing**: CFG only in a mid-trajectory window gives Sync-C 6.81 vs 7.13 for all-step CFG and 6.14 for none, with better FVD (119.88 vs 138.32).
  - Source: [Lip Forcing, arXiv 2606.11180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11180)

### Inferences
- [I] **Mechanism for SANG.**
  - With audio-only CFG, v = v_u + γ(v_c − v_u), where v_u keeps reference and prefix but drops audio.
  - For a near-straight rectified flow, the sampled mouth trajectory is roughly m_u + γ·(m_c − m_u).
  - So the audio-specific deviation from the audio-null mouth trajectory is multiplied by about γ. At γ = 2 an inflation of about 1.5x is expected whenever m_u carries a moderate "generic speaking" trajectory.
  - This matches SANG's numbers: std ratio 1.06 at training validation vs 1.52 at inference, assuming the validation was un-guided.
- [I] **Over-hard closures are the motion analogue of CFG "oversaturation".**
  - At a bilabial, the conditional prediction approaches closure (real ≈ 0.039), while the audio-null branch predicts a mid-openness mouth.
  - Extrapolating 2·m_c − m_u overshoots past closure. That is consistent with a median minimum of 0.005, which is probably a floor where the readout saturates at lip contact or compression.
  - Prediction: closure depth at /p b m/ should scale with γ and with the openness of the reference frame, because v_u is anchored to the reference and prefix mouth state.
  - Cheap diagnostic: bin clips by reference-frame openness and plot bilabial minimum openness per bin.
- [I] **Why corr stays at 0.65.** Correlation is scale-invariant, so CFG helps it only by raising the share of the audio-driven part relative to audio-independent variance. It also amplifies errors in (v_c − v_u).
  - Expectation: corr rises from γ = 1 to about 1.5 and then flattens or falls (FLOAT: only 0.055 LSE-D from γ 1 → 2), while std rises monotonically.
  - The fix is therefore two knobs: pick γ for correlation, then calibrate amplitude separately (see Q2).
- [I] **Eye std 1.24x.** The audio-null branch also removes audio-correlated eye and brow motion, so CFG inflates them too. This argues for per-region γ, with eyes and brows near 1.0.
- [I] **Arithmetic for the target metric.** Take real σ = 1, gen σ = 1.52 and ρ = 0.65, ignoring mean offset.
  - CCC = 2ρσ_g/(1 + σ_g²) = 1.976/3.310 = **0.597**.
  - Fixing only the amplitude to σ_g = 1 gives CCC = 0.65.
  - The per-frame MSE of normalised openness drops from 1.334 to 0.70 (−48%).
  - Amplitude calibration is therefore worth ~+0.05 CCC and halves the openness MSE. Closing the correlation gap to the renderer ceiling (0.82–0.85) is the larger remaining prize.

### Gaps
- No motion-space paper found that plots lip or mouth amplitude against the audio guidance scale. LeapTalk (pixel) plots only head-pose std. DiffPoseTalk has only an on/off ablation with unstated scales.
- AVTR-1's per-region weights and truncation are described but not ablated or published numerically.
- OmniHuman's CFG-annealing passage could not be confirmed in the HTML full text. It rests on a search extract only.

## Q2. Calibrating amplitude without losing sync (CFG rescale, guidance interval, APG, per-region guidance, lower γ, dynamic thresholding, amplitude/distribution losses)

### Takeaway
The image-diffusion literature offers three plug-in fixes that target the exact failure "guidance inflates magnitude". None has been tested in a talking-head motion space in any paper found. All three are inference-only for SANG:
- **CFG rescale** (match the std of the guided prediction to the conditional one).
- **APG** (drop the component of the guidance update parallel to the conditional prediction, which "primarily causes oversaturation").
- **Guidance interval** (guidance is harmful at high noise and unnecessary at low noise).

Motion-space systems add two more: per-region guidance (AVTR-1) and noise truncation (AVTR-1, τ = 1.2). Training-side amplitude losses are not indicated for SANG, because it is already calibrated at training time (1.06).

### Cited Findings
- [C] (image, mechanism) **CFG rescale** (Lin et al., "Common Diffusion Noise Schedules and Sample Steps are Flawed").
  - Formula: σ_pos = std(x_pos), σ_cfg = std(x_cfg); x_rescaled = x_cfg·σ_pos/σ_cfg; x_final = φ·x_rescaled + (1−φ)·x_cfg.
  - Recommended φ = 0.7 at w = 7.5. The ablation over φ ∈ [0, 1] is visual only, with the best range 0.5–0.75.
  - Rationale: "when [guidance weight] is large, the scale of the resulting x_cfg is very big, causing the image over-exposure problem".
  - No FID is reported for the rescale alone. The combined fixes give SD2.1-base FID 23.76 → 21.66.
  - Source: [Lin et al., arXiv 2305.08891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08891)
- [C] (image, mechanism) **APG** (Sadat, Hilliges, Weber; ICLR 2025).
  - The CFG update is split into components parallel and orthogonal to the conditional prediction. "The parallel component primarily causes oversaturation, while the orthogonal component enhances image quality."
  - APG down-weights the parallel part and adds a rescaling (norm-bound) step and "reverse momentum".
  - Claimed "improved FID, recall, and saturation scores while maintaining precision comparable to CFG".
  - Source: [APG, arXiv 2410.02416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02416)
- [M] (image, mechanism) **Guidance interval** (Kynkäänniemi et al.): "guidance is clearly harmful toward the beginning of the chain (high noise levels), largely unnecessary toward the end (low noise levels), and only beneficial in the middle".
  - ImageNet-512 FID improves "from 1.81 to 1.40" (EDM2). It also helps SDXL.
  - Source: [arXiv 2404.07724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07724)
- [M] (pixel) **Lip Forcing**: the audio-sync analogue of the interval idea. Mid-band CFG keeps most of the Sync-C gain (6.81 vs 7.13 all-step, 6.14 none) with better FVD.
  - Source: [arXiv 2606.11180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11180) (companion note)
- [C] (motion, same 42-d space) **AVTR-1**:
  - Per-region guidance residuals over rotation, brow, eyes and mouth, against a past-only baseline.
  - Initial-noise truncation to [−1.2, 1.2] with correlated noise.
  - Released runtime uses one weight per condition across regions.
  - Source: [arXiv 2609.22913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913)
- [M] (pixel) **LeapTalk** picks a low default scale, α = 1.6, because amplitude (pose std) scales steeply with α (Tab. 7 above).
  - Source: [arXiv 2608.00079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00079)
- [C] (pixel) **OmniHuman-1**: CFG annealing, i.e. decreasing γ over sampling, to keep lip-sync while removing artefacts from over-strong guidance. No numbers; see Q1 caveat.
  - Source: [arXiv 2502.01061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01061)
- [M] (motion, amplitude-matching loss precedent) **DiffPoseTalk** evaluates the mouth-opening difference vs GT (MOD) and an FDD that compares per-vertex temporal std between prediction and GT. These are the natural amplitude-matching targets if one ever wants a training loss.
  - Source: [arXiv 2310.00434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00434)
- [C] (3D) **CorrTalk**: mapping "single-level speech features to the entire facial animation ... overlook[s] the differences in facial activity intensity leading to overly smoothed facial movements". It proposes separate strong-activity (mouth) and weak-activity branches.
  - Relevance: the mouth and the rest of the face need different amplitude handling, which argues for per-region control.
  - Source: [CorrTalk, arXiv 2310.11295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11295)

### Inferences
- [I] **Motion-space CFG rescale for SANG** (inference-only).
  1. At each Euler step, form the clean estimates x̂1_c = x_t + (1−t)·v_c and x̂1_cfg = x_t + (1−t)·v_cfg.
  2. For the mouth dims (6 keypoints × 3) and the eye dims, compute the temporal std over the 64-frame window per dim, around the per-window mean.
  3. Rescale the deviation of x̂1_cfg to σ(x̂1_c), blend with φ ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 1.0}, and convert back to a velocity.
  - Unlike a global post-hoc scale, this keeps the guided direction, which carries the correlation gain, while pinning amplitude to the conditional model, which was calibrated at training (1.06).
- [I] **Motion-space APG.**
  - Split Δ = v_c − v_u, or better Δx̂1, into the component parallel to x̂1_c's deviation from its window mean and the orthogonal remainder.
  - Down-weight the parallel part (η ∈ {0, 0.5}).
  - In motion terms, the parallel component is "more of the same trajectory", which is pure amplitude inflation. The orthogonal component changes timing and shape. So APG targets over-articulation more surgically than lowering γ.
- [I] **Guidance interval or annealing.**
  - Apply audio CFG only for mid-t, e.g. t ∈ [0.2, 0.7] with t = 0 as noise and t = 1 as data, or use a γ(t) that decays to 1 near t = 1.
  - The low-noise steps set the fine extremes (closure depth). Guidance there is "largely unnecessary" (2404.07724) and is the most likely source of the 0.005 floor.
- [I] **Per-region γ.** SANG's output is already region-indexed (3 angles plus keypoint coordinates), so per-dim γ is trivial.
  - Start with γ_mouth ∈ {1.25, 1.5}, γ_eyes = γ_brow = 1.0, γ_pose ∈ {1.0, 1.5}.
  - This should fix the eye std (1.24x) without touching blink rate, which is already matched.
- [I] **Noise truncation** (τ ≈ 1.2) and a **dynamic-threshold-style clamp** of x̂1 lip coordinates to training-data percentiles (e.g. p0.5–p99.5 of speaker-normalised coordinates) directly bound closure overshoot. Cheap, but may clip real extremes, so check the distribution tail vs GT.
- [I] **Training-side amplitude losses** (window-std matching, MOD-style) are unlikely to help while the training-time std ratio is 1.06 and the velocity ratio is 0.975. The inflation arises at inference. Revisit only if the γ = 1 sweep still shows std > 1.2.
- [I] **Longer-term, if high γ is needed for correlation:** train the model to be audio-specific without guidance, via DPO with CFG-inflated samples as losers (Q4), or distil a calibrated guided model. Neither has a motion-space precedent with numbers.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies CFG rescale, APG or guidance intervals to talking-head motion (keypoint, 3DMM or latent) with sync or amplitude numbers.
- No quantitative ablation found for AVTR-1's per-region weights or truncation.

## Q3. Audio representation choices with measured lip-sync gains (WavLM layers, Whisper, HuBERT, AV-HuBERT, phoneme posteriors, window width)

### Takeaway
Audio representation affects **correlation**, not amplitude. It is the main lever for moving corr 0.65 toward the 0.82–0.85 renderer ceiling.
- Measured encoder swaps move Sync-C by up to ~3.4 points in a LivePortrait-keypoint model (Teller: Whisper vs codec features, companion note).
- AV-HuBERT and phoneme-aware features lead elsewhere.
- Current motion-space systems mostly use HuBERT (AVTR-1, Ditto, DiffPoseTalk).
- Probing shows articulatory (EMA) content in SSL models peaks mid-network and "a few layers before the last", not at the final layer. That supports replacing WavLM-last with a learned layer mix, but no talking-head paper ablates WavLM layers.

### Cited Findings
- (companion note, verified) **Measured encoder effects:**
  - Teller Tab. 3 (motion tokens on LivePortrait keypoints): Whisper Sync-C 7.696 vs funcodec 4.286.
  - PASE table (NeRF renderer): AV-HuBERT 8.0136, HuBERT 7.6599, Whisper 3.3416, PASE 8.7098 LSE-C.
  - SyncDiff: AV-HuBERT 7.05 vs DeepSpeech 6.11 LSE-C; partially fine-tuning the top 3 AV-HuBERT layers is best.
  - KDTalker Tab. 7: 64-frame window best.
  - Sources: [Teller 2503.18429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18429), [PASE 2504.05803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05803), [SyncDiff 2503.13371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13371)
- [C] (probing) **Pasad et al.**: in HuBERT, WavLM and AV-HuBERT, "the phonetic and word information appears to be concentrated toward higher layers than for the other ('autoencoder-like') models".
  - Single-layer vs all-layer downstream tests cover W2V2 and HuBERT only: "the best single-layer ASR performance for W2V2-small, HuBERT-small, and HuBERT-large is better than that of all-layers (by 4-9% relative)".
  - No WavLM peak-layer index is given numerically.
  - Source: [arXiv 2211.03929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03929)
- [M] (probing, articulatory) **Linear frame-wise probes from SSL layers to EMA articulator trajectories:**
  - "two local peaks in scores through the layers: the first one around the middle and the second one a few layers before the last layer".
  - "HuBERT Large layer-11 shows the highest score among 24 layers" (overall 0.81).
  - "scores differ dramatically by models near the last layers where the effects of learning objective are strong".
  - Vertical (Y) articulator coordinates are decoded well; lip X-coordinates poorly. Lip aperture is mainly vertical.
  - Source: [arXiv 2210.11723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11723). Title recalled as "Evidence of Vocal Tract Articulation in Self-Supervised Learning of Speech"; the fetched page did not display it.
- [M] (3D motion) **Wav2Sem**: "near-homophonic syllables show significant coupling in the feature space of existing self-supervised models, which leads to the averaging of near-homophonic syllables in the subsequent lip shape generation process".
  - A semantic-decoupling plug-in improves FaceFormer LVE 4.1090 → 3.9891 and FDD 4.6675 → 4.5317, and CodeTalker LVE 3.9445 → 3.8838 (small, ~1.5–3%).
  - Source: [Wav2Sem, arXiv 2505.23290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23290)
- [C] (3D motion) **CorrTalk** uses a weighted hierarchical (multi-layer) speech encoder to drive strong (mouth) vs weak facial activity. Its ablation numbers were not retrieved because the PDF was too large to fetch.
  - Source: [arXiv 2310.11295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11295)
- [C] (motion) **Encoders in current motion-space systems:**
  - AVTR-1: HuBERT-Large, 1024-d @ 50 Hz ([2609.22913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913)).
  - Ditto: HuBERT with 0.4 s streaming units ([2411.19509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)).
  - DiffPoseTalk: HuBERT with a trainable transformer ([2310.00434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00434)).
  - KDTalker: Wav2Lip audio encoder ([2503.12963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)).
  - OmniHuman-1 (pixel): "multi-scale" wav2vec features concatenated "with audio features from adjacent timestamps" ([2502.01061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01061)).
  - None ablates the layer.
- [M] (sync-expert evidence on audio features) **UniSync**: HuBERT beats Mel spectrograms as the audio side of a sync expert for every visual representation. Examples: 3DMM 91.40% vs 89.00% max accuracy; landmarks 89.00% vs 86.23%.
  - Source: [UniSync, arXiv 2503.16357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16357)

### Inferences
- [I] **Window width.** Folding 2 WavLM ticks per frame and then applying a centred k = 5 Conv1d at 25 fps gives a direct receptive field of ±2 frames = ±80 ms. This matches FLOAT's ±2 frames and Sonic's 0.2 s.
  - The 64-frame bidirectional transformer already reaches wider context through attention. Widening the conv alone is therefore a low-expected-value change.
  - A per-frame cross-attention to a ±6-frame (±240 ms) raw-feature window is a more targeted test of whether direct local audio access limits correlation.
  - No talking-head paper found gives a clean window-width ablation.
- [I] **Layer choice.**
  - Cheapest: a learned softmax-weighted sum of all 25 WavLM-large hidden states (SUPERB-style). Alternative: fixed mid-to-upper layers, e.g. concatenate layers ~12 and ~20.
  - The articulatory-probe peaks (mid and near-top, not final) motivate this. Pasad's result that a single good layer can beat an all-layer sum means both variants are worth one run each.
  - Cost: re-extract features (disk ~2–25x if caching all layers; cache 3–4 chosen layers instead) plus one retrain.
- [I] **Encoder swap.** HuBERT-Large is the modal choice in motion-space systems (AVTR-1, Ditto, DiffPoseTalk). Whisper has the only measured win on LivePortrait-keypoint motion (Teller). AV-HuBERT leads on lip-aware sync (PASE, SyncDiff). For a single retrain budget: Whisper encoder first, AV-HuBERT audio stream second.
- [I] **Phoneme/viseme auxiliary head** on the audio pathway (CTC or frame labels from forced alignment) targets Wav2Sem's "averaging of near-homophonic syllables". The expected gain is small (Wav2Sem ~1.5–3% LVE) but it is cheap.
- [I] None of these fixes amplitude. Do them after the Q2 calibration, and judge by correlation/CCC at a fixed, calibrated amplitude.

### Gaps
- No paper found that ablates WavLM last layer vs intermediate or weighted layers in any audio-to-motion talking-head model.
- No motion-space window-width ablation found beyond KDTalker's sequence length.
- The CorrTalk hierarchical-feature ablation was not retrieved.

## Q4. Motion-space sync supervision and reward/DPO fine-tuning with amplitude safeguards

### Takeaway
Sync experts and sync losses on compact geometry work: a HuBERT+3DMM sync expert reaches 91.40% vs 93.25% for RGB (UniSync). A mesh-to-speech loss gives +0.07 lip PCC and +0.067 lip CCC (THUNDER).

The key motion-space caution is that a **generic lip expert inflates Sync-conf while worsening mouth-landmark error** (DreamTalk: Sync 2.63 → 4.51 but M-LMD 3.07 → 3.42). This is the same exaggeration bias FantasyTalking2 documents for Sync-C.

Motion-space DPO exists: Avatar Forcing uses synthetic losers, but it increased motion variance (Var 1.408 → 1.734), which is the wrong direction for SANG unless the losers are chosen to be the over-articulated samples. Every sync reward should be paired with an amplitude or CCC guard.

### Cited Findings
- (companion note, verified) Numbers held there, not repeated:
  - LatentSync (pixel SyncNet, Sync-conf 4.6 → 8.9).
  - Learn2Talk SyncNet3D (VOCASET LSE-C 6.286 → 9.838 on its own 3D SyncNet; BIWI LVE worsens 4.6971 → 5.0003).
  - arXiv 2503.20308 speech-mesh perceptual loss.
  - DEMO (InfoNCE inside the motion AE; no ablation of it; non-standard LSE-D ~238–246).
  - Playmate2, Hallo4, FantasyTalking2 TLPO, FlowPortrait.
- [M] (sync expert on non-pixel inputs) **UniSync accuracy on LRS2** (max / avg):

  | Audio + visual | Max | Avg |
  |---|---|---|
  | HuBERT + RGB | 93.25% | 90.09% |
  | **HuBERT + 3DMM** | **91.40%** | **89.11%** |
  | Mel + RGB | 91.31% | 87.23% |
  | Mel + 3DMM | 89.00% | 85.93% |
  | **HuBERT + landmarks** | **89.00%** | **85.26%** |
  | HuBERT + face parsing | 86.04% | 82.50% |
  | Mel + landmarks | 86.23% | 82.39% |

  - Recipe: margin-based contrastive BCE with margins (0.3, 0.7) for same-speaker vs cross-speaker negatives, 20% cross-speaker negatives, 0.2 s windows, batch 64.
  - As an RGB loss in pixel generators:
    - Wav2Lip on LRS2: LSE-D 7.521 → 6.647, LSE-C 6.406 → 6.842.
    - GeneFace: LSE-D 9.809 → 9.396, LSE-C 4.765 → 5.294.
  - No landmark or 3DMM variant was used as a loss.
  - Source: [UniSync, arXiv 2503.16357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16357)
- [M] (3D motion, diffusion) **THUNDER** (Daněček et al.): a mesh-to-speech model regresses audio from generated FLAME animation, and its error is a differentiable loss (w_m2s = 1, alongside L_rec + L_vel).
  - Tab. 2, with vs without M2S: LVE 0.879 → **0.802 cm**; lip CCC 0.359 → **0.426**; lip PCC 0.568 → **0.639**; lip DTW 0.329 → **0.29 cm**.
  - "models with M2S improve all lip-sync metrics at slight expense of diversity" (S-DIV-U 0.0419 → 0.0322).
  - Motivation: "deterministic models produce high-quality lip-sync but without rich expressions, whereas stochastic models generate diverse expressions but with lower lip-sync quality".
  - Source: [THUNDER, arXiv 2504.13386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13386)
- [M] (motion, 3DMM) **DreamTalk Tab. IV (MEAD)**, lip expert on **3DMM mouth-mesh vertices** (MLP motion encoder, 1D-conv audio encoder):

  | Variant | SSIM | F-LMD | M-LMD | Sync_conf |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | w/o lip expert | 0.85 | 1.90 | 3.07 | 2.63 |
  | Unconditional (generic) lip expert | 0.83 | 2.19 | 3.42 | 4.51 |
  | Style-aware lip expert (full) | 0.86 | 1.93 | 2.91 | 3.78 |

  - The generic expert gives the largest Sync_conf but the worst mouth-landmark error. The authors say it drives "face motions in a uniform style" and "reduces the intensity of expressions".
  - Conditioning the expert on the speaker's style (reference) trades 0.73 Sync_conf for the best M-LMD.
  - Source: [DreamTalk, arXiv 2312.09767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09767)
- [M] (motion-latent DPO) **Avatar Forcing**: L_ft = L_DF + λ·L_DPO.
  - Winners are GT; losers are synthetic samples "conditioned solely on the avatar audio", with user conditions dropped.
  - Tab. 4, without → with DPO: rPCC-Exp 0.042 → 0.003; rPCC-Pose 0.146 → 0.036 (both lower = better); SID 2.236 → 2.442; **Var 1.408 → 1.734**.
  - There are no sync metrics for this ablation and no amplitude safeguard.
  - Source: [Avatar Forcing, arXiv 2601.00664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00664)
- [M]/[C] (pixel) **FantasyTalking2**: Sync-C favours exaggeration. It matches human preference 72.34% of the time vs 86.94% for their reward model. The lip-sync expert is constrained with a MediaPipe lip mask, and there is no explicit amplitude term.
  - Source: [arXiv 2508.11255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11255)

### Inferences
- [I] **Build a keypoint sync expert first as an evaluator**, UniSync-style.
  - Inputs: SANG's 6 mouth keypoints × 3 over 5–16 frames, paired with HuBERT (or the chosen audio) features. Use margin loss with cross-speaker negatives on the existing TalkVid cache.
  - UniSync's 3DMM/landmark numbers suggest ~89–91% accuracy is reachable.
  - This gives a motion-space LSE that is independent of the broken pixel SyncNet pipeline.
- [I] **Only then use it as a loss, and in DreamTalk's "style-aware" form.** Condition the expert on the reference/canonical keypoints or speaker mouth statistics, so it does not reward a generic, larger mouth.
  - Guard with a CCC or std-ratio term on lip openness and a bilabial-closure-depth check.
- [I] **Mesh-to-speech analogue.** A keypoint-to-audio-feature regressor (predict WavLM/HuBERT features from lip-keypoint windows) as an auxiliary loss is THUNDER's recipe. Its measured gain (+0.07 PCC) is the size of improvement SANG needs toward the ceiling. It is costlier than a contrastive expert.
- [I] **DPO for SANG, amplitude-safe.** Winners are GT motion windows. Losers are the model's own over-guided samples (γ = 2.5–3) or wrong-audio samples.
  - This teaches audio-specificity that CFG currently supplies, and explicitly penalises inflated amplitude.
  - Avatar Forcing shows the synthetic-loser recipe works in a motion-latent DiT with the same sampler (CFG 2, 10 Euler NFEs). With inflated losers, its Var-increasing effect should flip sign.
  - Playmate2/Hallo4-style SyncNet best-vs-worst pairs are a second option, but only with a reward of the form Sync − λ·|log(std_gen/std_GT)|.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains a sync expert on LivePortrait implicit keypoints, or uses a landmark or 3DMM sync expert as a generator loss with 2D LSE numbers.
- No motion-space DPO paper found that reports lip-sync metrics or an amplitude safeguard.
- DEMO's InfoNCE remains un-ablated (companion note).

## Q5. What the best motion-space systems report for lip amplitude and sync, how they evaluate, and a ranked plan for SANG

### Takeaway
Leading motion-space talking-head systems report pixel metrics only (FID, FVD, CSIM, Sync-C/D), and none reports a lip-amplitude statistic:
- KDTalker: LSE-C 7.326 vs real 8.243.
- AVTR-1: LSE-C 3.28, LSE-D 7.08 in an interactive setting.
- Ditto.

Amplitude-aware lip metrics exist in the 3D-mesh literature: MOD, FDD, lip CCC and PCC, LVE, DTW, SLCC. SANG should adopt the CCC/MOD-style pair, because Sync-C alone rewards exactly SANG's failure mode.

### Cited Findings
- [M] (motion, LivePortrait keypoints) **KDTalker Tab. 1 (HDTF)**:

  | Method | LSE-C | LSE-D | Diversity |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Real video | 8.243 | 6.929 | 0.639 |
  | KDTalker | 7.326 | 7.548 | 0.760 |
  | SadTalker | 7.121 | 7.813 | 0.494 |
  | Real3DPortrait | 7.004 | 7.804 | 0.118 |
  | AniTalker | 7.107 | 8.077 | 0.374 |
  | AniPortrait | 3.438 | 10.946 | 0.353 |

  - Diversity = std of head-motion feature embeddings. There is no lip-amplitude metric.
  - Source: [KDTalker, arXiv 2503.12963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12963)
- [M] (motion, 42-d LivePortrait motion) **AVTR-1 Tab. 5**: FID 14.3, FVD 76.8, CSIM 0.94, LSE-D 7.08, LSE-C 3.28 (interactive/dyadic benchmark; GT row not extracted). Listening metrics rPCC, PFD, SID and Var are in Tab. 6. No mouth-amplitude metric.
  - Source: [arXiv 2609.22913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913)
- [C] (motion) **Ditto** reports FID, FVD, CSIM, Sync-C and Sync-D only.
  - Source: [arXiv 2411.19509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19509)
- [M] (3D) **Amplitude-aware lip metrics:**
  - DiffPoseTalk:
    - LVE = "maximum L2 error across all lip vertices for each frame".
    - FDD compares "the standard deviation of each upper face vertex's motion over time between the prediction and the ground truth".
    - **MOD** = "average difference in the size of the mouth opening between the prediction and ground truth".
    - Source: [arXiv 2310.00434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00434)
  - THUNDER: **lip CCC** (penalises amplitude or offset mismatch as well as low correlation), lip PCC, lip DTW, LVE, FDD.
    - Source: [arXiv 2504.13386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13386)
  - arXiv 2503.20308 defines three perceptual criteria:
    - Temporal Synchronization → MTM, in ms.
    - Lip Readability → PLRS, a cosine in a speech-mesh space.
    - **Expressiveness → SLCC**, the Pearson r between speech intensity and lip displacement. VOCASET GT 0.34; FaceFormer 0.26, CodeTalker 0.38, SelfTalk 0.41.
    - Human study: 82.6% preferred intensity-matched lips over perfectly synced but unexpressive ones (17.4%), "even if they exceed the established maximum acceptable asynchrony by twice" (100 ms vs 50 ms).
    - Source: [arXiv 2503.20308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20308)
- [C] (pixel) **Soft-DTW re-formulation** of LPIPS, CSIM, Sync-D and pose (7-d) / expression (63-d) trajectory distances, for robustness to "slight timing shifts, different speaking speeds, and stylistic variations". It does not quantify SyncNet's exaggeration bias.
  - Source: [arXiv 2606.01031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01031)
- [M] (motion, 3DMM) **DreamTalk's metric suite** pairs Sync_conf with mouth-landmark distance (M-LMD). That is exactly the pairing that exposes the generic lip expert's Sync gain as a mouth-accuracy loss (Q4 table).
  - Source: [arXiv 2312.09767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09767)

### Inferences
- [I] **SANG eval panel.** Keep the existing 300-clip / 42-speaker set. Report all of the following on lip openness, speaker-normalised:
  1. Std ratio (target 0.9–1.1).
  2. PCC and **CCC** (current CCC ≈ 0.597; ceiling ≈ renderer 0.82–0.85).
  3. MOD-style mean |open_gen − open_real|.
  4. Per-phone-class openness percentiles: bilabial p10 and median minimum (target ≈ 0.039), and open vowels p90.
  5. Lip-velocity std ratio.
  6. Soft-DTW lip distance.
  7. An SLCC-analogue (loudness–openness r) compared with the real clip's own value.
  8. Eye std ratio and blink rate.
  9. audio_gain.
  - Once LSE can be measured, report it only next to the std ratio and CCC.
- [I] Add a keypoint-space sync expert (Q4) as a renderer-free LSE, and validate it on real motion. Its score on real data is the ceiling.

### Gaps
- No motion-space talking-head paper found that reports a lip-opening amplitude statistic vs GT (std ratio or MOD) for LivePortrait-keypoint generators.
- AVTR-1's GT LSE row and any amplitude metric were not extracted.
- SyncNet's exaggeration bias is documented qualitatively and by human-agreement rate (72.34%), but not as an LSE-vs-amplitude curve.

## Ranked plan for SANG ([I] unless noted; cheapest first)

Hypothesis scorecard:
- **H1, CFG 2.0 over-articulates: strongly supported.**
  - Mechanism: 2305.08891 and 2410.02416.
  - Pixel evidence: LeapTalk pose std grows ~linearly with scale.
  - Motion evidence: DiffPoseTalk MOD worsens with CFG.
  - SANG's own gap: train std 1.06 vs inference 1.52.
- **H2, WavLM last layer suboptimal: plausible.** Supported by articulatory probing (2210.11723), but not ablated for talking heads. It affects correlation, not amplitude.
- **H3, ±80 ms too narrow: weak.** It matches FLOAT and Sonic, and the 64-frame transformer already sees wider context.
- **H4, region-balanced or velocity loss favours large motion: contradicted.** The training-time std ratio is 1.06 and the velocity ratio is 0.975.
- **H5, steps or EMA: minor.** FLOAT and KDTalker show 5–10 steps are slightly better for sync than more. There is no EMA evidence. Test within the sweep.

**Tier 0: measurement, hours.**
1. Check whether the training-time validation used CFG.
2. Add the Q5 panel (std ratio, CCC, MOD, bilabial closure depth, eye std).
3. Run the reference-openness diagnostic: bilabial minimum openness binned by the openness of the reference frame. This predicts whether the audio-null baseline is anchoring the closure overshoot.

**Tier 1: inference-only, minutes per setting on 300 clips.**
1. **γ sweep** {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.5} × Euler steps {5, 10} × {EMA, raw}.
   - Expectation: std ratio falls roughly linearly toward ~1.0–1.1 at γ = 1, and closure depth rises toward 0.039.
   - Pick γ by CCC with audio_gain ≥ its current level. Evidence: LeapTalk Tab. 7 [M, pixel]; DiffPoseTalk Tab. 1 [M, motion]; FLOAT Tab. 6 [M, motion].
2. **Per-region γ** (AVTR-1-style): γ_mouth ∈ {1.25, 1.5, 2.0}, γ_eyes = γ_brow = 1.0, γ_pose ∈ {1.0, 1.5}. Targets the eye std of 1.24x. Evidence: [C, motion, 2609.22913].
3. **Guidance interval or annealing**: audio CFG only for mid-t (e.g. t ∈ [0.2, 0.7]), or γ(t) decaying to 1 at low noise. Targets the closure floor while keeping the correlation gain. Evidence: 2404.07724 [M, image]; Lip Forcing [M, pixel]; OmniHuman [C, pixel].
4. **Motion-space CFG rescale** (φ ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 1.0}) on the x̂1 temporal std of the mouth and eye dims, and **APG** (parallel component η ∈ {0, 0.5}, norm clamp). These keep a higher γ for correlation while pinning amplitude to the conditional prediction. Evidence: 2305.08891 [C, image]; 2410.02416 [C, image]. Untested in motion space.
5. **Noise truncation** τ ∈ {1.2, 1.5} (AVTR-1) and a percentile clamp on lip coordinates of x̂1. Also run a post-hoc per-region deviation scale (1/1.52 on the mouth) purely as a bound on what calibration alone can buy (expected CCC ≈ 0.65 at the current correlation).

**Tier 2: re-extract features plus one retrain each; targets correlation 0.65 → ceiling.**
1. **Audio features**: a learned weighted sum of WavLM-large layers, or a fixed mid/upper-layer concat; then the Whisper encoder (Teller [M, motion]), then HuBERT-Large (AVTR-1, Ditto, DiffPoseTalk) or the AV-HuBERT audio stream (PASE, SyncDiff [M, pixel/NeRF]).
2. **Per-frame cross-attention** to a ±240 ms raw audio window, plus an optional phoneme/viseme CTC auxiliary head (Wav2Sem [M, 3D], small gains).
3. Evaluate at the Tier-1 calibrated guidance setting, judging by CCC and correlation.

**Tier 3: fine-tunes; days.**
1. **Keypoint sync expert** (UniSync recipe [M]: 3DMM 91.40%, landmarks 89.00% with HuBERT). Use it first as an evaluator, then as a style-aware loss with a CCC/std guard. DreamTalk [M, motion] shows that a generic expert raises Sync 2.63 → 4.51 but worsens M-LMD 3.07 → 3.42.
2. **Amplitude-safe DPO**: GT winners vs the model's over-guided (γ 2.5–3) or wrong-audio losers. Follows the Avatar Forcing synthetic-loser recipe [M, motion latent], with loser choice inverted to penalise inflation. Aim: reach the γ≈2 correlation at γ≈1.
3. **THUNDER-style keypoint-to-speech auxiliary loss** [M, 3D: +0.07 lip PCC, +0.067 lip CCC] if the sync expert plateaus.
